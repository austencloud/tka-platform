# Interaction Design Research: Making Learning Feel Like Play

Research compiled 2026-03-08 from analysis of Brilliant.org, Khan Academy, Duolingo, 3Blue1Brown, Memrise, and 2025-2026 neuroscience findings on learning and reward.

---

## Platform Analysis

### Brilliant.org — Earned Insight

Core mechanic: interactive problem sequences. Problems before explanations. The dopamine trigger is the moment you realize you can solve something you thought was beyond you.

- **Problem-first pedagogy.** Encounter the problem before the explanation. Creates "productive struggle" gap. Solutions via insight trigger hippocampal bursts that dramatically improve long-term recall.
- **Scaffolding that disappears.** Early problems include visual aids. Practice strips them away. Training wheels come off unnoticed.
- **Celebration flourishes.** Developed with ustwo. Correct answers trigger whimsical animations with sound and haptics. Brief (sub-second) — enough for dopamine hit without breaking concentration.
- **Optimal next problem prediction.** Keeps you in the flow channel.

### Duolingo — The Addiction Engine

- **Streaks.** 7-day streak users are 3.6x more likely to stay engaged long-term.
- **Hearts system.** Five mistakes and you're locked out. Creates urgency and careful engagement.
- **Variable XP rewards.** Standard + unpredictable bonuses. Brain never knows what's coming — sustains dopamine during anticipation.
- **Weekly leagues.** ~30 users compete. Lesson completion rose 25% after introduction.
- **22 identified gamification elements** in total.

### Khan Academy — Mastery as Reward

- **Mastery progression.** Practiced → Level 1 → Level 2 → Mastered. Not binary pass/fail.
- **Energy points measure effort, not ability.** Reduces anxiety.
- **Personal progress over competition.** You vs. yesterday's you.

### 3Blue1Brown — Visual Intuition

- **Concrete before abstract.** Specific examples before general frameworks.
- **Continuous transformation.** Animate the change, not before/after snapshots. The motion IS the understanding.
- **Programmatic precision.** A rotation is a rotation — visual never lies about the concept.

### Memrise — Memory as Game

- **Spaced repetition.** Surfaces items just before forgetting curve drops.
- **Near-miss rescue timing.** Reviewing at the exact moment you're about to forget, and getting it right.

## The Science

### Micro-Reward Timing (2025)

When rewards are spaced 5-10 interactions apart, subjects needed **one-tenth** as many pairings to learn compared to every 30-60 seconds. Interval between cue and reward controls how much each instance updates the brain's model.

**Implication:** Don't celebrate every correct tap. Space big celebrations. Let 3-4 correct answers build quiet momentum, deliver reward on the 5th.

Combining spaced repetition with gamification produces **3x better retention** than either alone.

### Animation and Retention

Animated training modules: **65% more retention** than traditional video. Interactive content increases attention by **40%** and recall by **25-30%**.

Key distinction: animation that SHOWS the transformation teaches. Animation that DECORATES entertains.

### Embodied Cognition and Touch (2025)

Technology-based embodied learning has statistically significant positive effect on outcomes. Physical manipulation of spatial concepts → stronger neural pathways than reading.

**Caveat:** High bodily engagement can create cognitive overload. Manipulation must be directly mapped to the concept — not arbitrary gesturing. Dragging a hand on a grid = good. Swiping through options = neutral.

### The Aha Moment

Insights via "aha moments" remembered **far better** than solutions via methodical reasoning. Flash of insight triggers hippocampal burst — greater intensity = greater memory boost.

**Design implication:** The insight must be earned. Productive struggle before the aha is the neural setup. Never explain what a learner can discover.

### Flow State

Flow occurs when challenge slightly exceeds current skill. Too easy = boredom. Too hard = anxiety. Balance is dynamic — as skill increases, challenge must increase to match.
