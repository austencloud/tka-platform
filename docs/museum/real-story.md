## The VTG Influence (What Actually Happened)

Austen started spinning in 2012, right after VTG 3 (the final section of Vulcan Tech Gospel) was released. He did NOT study VTG directly at the outset. The influence was indirect:

- Went to festivals
- Took classes on anti-spin, isolation, flowers
- Watched videos other people had posted
- Learned from people who had studied VTG, or who were adjacent to people who had studied VTG

The four modes of timing and direction reached him through community osmosis, not through sitting down with VTG as a curriculum. He absorbed the concepts by being in the ecosystem during the period when VTG's terminology was becoming the common language of the tech-spinning community.

**Key distinction:** VTG's influence on TKA was not "I studied VTG and built on it." It was "I was adjacent to a community shaped by VTG, and the foundational concepts (timing, direction, the four modes) were in the water supply."

### What "VTG" Means in Practice (2026)

Austen observes that when people say "VTG" in 2026, they're usually referring to the four modes of timing and direction specifically. But VTG as originally conceived and as represented in the VTG app was broader:

- A number system representing combinations of rotations, timing, and direction
- A system of pedagogy with specific terminology
- A classification framework

The four modes became the part that stuck in the community's collective vocabulary. The rest of the system is less commonly referenced by the average practitioner.

## The Type System Evolution

### Original Conception: Three Worlds

When Austen first started mapping the system, he split the types differently:

- What is now Type 1 was originally split into two separate categories
- What is now Type 2 was originally called Type 3
- He conceived of three "worlds":
  1. **The Alpha-Beta World** - hands at established positions
  2. **The Gamma World** - hands at the gamma position
  3. **Traveling Between** - moving between those two worlds

### The Dash Discovery

The revision happened when Austen formalized the concept of the **dash**: a hand moving directly across the center to the opposite grid point. He didn't invent the move (people were doing it long before he entered the scene), but he:

1. Gave it a name
2. Specified it precisely: a hand moves in a straight line through center to the opposite point
3. Recognized it as fundamentally distinct from shifts

**Why dashes are different from shifts:** A dash traces a straight line through center, so turning behavior operates in a Cartesian space (the rotation axis stays fixed along that line). Shifts curve around the grid perimeter, so the rotation behavior changes with the path. Different geometry, different visual results when turns are applied.

### The Revision to Motion Types

The dash discovery forced a rethinking of the entire type system. Austen realized the proper way to categorize letter types was by the combination of motion types (shift, dash, static) rather than by the three-worlds model. This was the right move architecturally, even though it meant restructuring what he'd already built.

**The current Type system (1-6)** is organized by motion type combination, not by positional world.

## The Pictograph-First Approach

Austen's process was NOT: study existing theory → build a system.

It was: map the entire possibility space using pictographs → recognize the depth of the structure → THEN go back and learn VTG to see where the connections were.

The pictographs came first. The theoretical framework for understanding what the pictographs showed came from making them and staring at them until the patterns emerged. The connection to VTG was recognized retroactively, not used as a starting point.

## The Elemental System

### The Four Elements (Community-Created)

Other people in the flow arts community (not Austen) developed a classification system mapping the four timing/direction modes to classical elements:

| Element | Mode |
|---------|------|
| **Fire** | Split time, opposite direction |
| **Water** | Split time, same direction |
| **Earth** | Together time, same direction |
| **Air** | Together time, opposite direction |

### Sun and Moon (Austen's Addition)

Austen extended this to include the quarter-time modes:

| Element | Mode |
|---------|------|
| **Sun** | Quarter time, same direction |
| **Moon** | Quarter time, opposite direction |

### Noel Yee's Position on Elements

Noel has expressed that the four modes of timing and direction are the bedrock and that the timing/direction terminology is the right way to talk about things. He's been supportive of people continuing to create new work but is firm that the terminology itself is what matters.

He does not prefer the elemental naming convention. Austen's perspective: the elements provide an additional associative layer that aids memorization and intuition. VTG is open-source and intended to be built upon.

### Pedagogical Ordering Difference

- **VTG** starts with together-same (Earth)
- **TKA** starts with split-same (Water)

Austen's reasoning for starting with split-same:
1. Split-same is visually clearer on the grid (props at opposite ends, arrows in different places, easy to see)
2. Weaves are split-same, and weaves are what most people learn first in real life
3. When holding props together (together-time), they occupy the same 2D space, which requires creative maneuvering that beginners may not understand yet
4. Taking two props at opposite ends and moving them in the same direction around the same circle point is literally the easiest thing to do and teaches the foundational concept of isolation

## Relationship to Other Systems

### Spin Science (Lorq)

Lorq's Spin Science system is, in Austen's assessment, closer to TKA's approach than traditional VTG because it leans heavily into visual snapshots of orientations. The flower/petal diagrams exhaustively catalog prop orientations across full cycles. It's deeply focused on clubs at its core.

Lorq personally taught Austen stabs using the Spin Science framework.

### The Broader Ecosystem

Multiple people have built classification and notation systems for flow arts:

- **VTG** (Noel Yee + Vulcan Lofts) - timing/direction classification
- **Spin Science** (Lorq) - orientation visualization, club-focused
- **QFT Notation** (Charlie Cushing + Drex) - written shorthand for poi patterns
- **9-Square Theory** (Charlie Cushing) - spatial grid for poi
- **CAPs** (Zaltymbunk et al.) - mathematical composite patterns

TKA builds on concepts that exist in all of these but is distinct in its approach: exhaustive pictographic mapping of the entire possibility space for dual-wielded static props.
