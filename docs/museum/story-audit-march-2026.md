## Top Strengths

1. **Original premise** — fake government archive about flow arts history. Nothing else like it.
2. **Two-voice tone system** — Order (institutional, sincere) vs. Scribe (warm, human). Comedy through contrast.
3. **The fiction-to-reality handoff** — game becomes real at the end. Player receives actual tool. Structural innovation.
4. **Nathan Fielder Rule** — joke never explained. OOGA as ancient sequence. Ada Lovelace inventing programming. Order vs. YouTube.
5. **Termination Clause** — same words, two readings (failure vs. completion). Video essay bait.
6. **Flow state in-joke** — 40,000 years of documented "symptoms" every flow artist recognizes as Thursday practice.
7. **The Seam** — architectural transition from Order → Scribe. Player feels it physically walking through.
8. **Software archaeology** — stone → papyrus → brass → CRT → web app. Medium changes, notation doesn't.
9. **The MEH room** — apathy as the real threat. Hardest to write, potentially strongest moment.
10. **Wax figures as thesis** — Order built robots to demonstrate without practicing. Stiff avatars = narrative, not limitation.

## Key Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Niche audience (flow arts) | -2 to -4% Steam rating | Institutional comedy works without domain knowledge. Flow arts layer is bonus, not requirement. |
| "Not a game" backlash | -3 to -5% Steam rating | **Add interactive elements. Draw from existing codebase.** This is the biggest addressable risk. |
| Chunky middle (Phase 2) | Player engagement drop | Vary exhibit types. Not just plaque → Scribe note → budget denial. Need different KINDS of interaction per era. |
| Voice acting quality | +/- 5% swing factor | Single biggest variable. Austen voicing everything. AI cloning for character variants. First project. |
| Handoff feels like marketing | -3 to -5% if it fails | Room 3 must EARN the moment emotionally. If it does, transcendent. If not, feels like an ad. |
| Solo developer scope | 5 phases, many rooms | Multi-year timeline. Build one phase at a time. Cave first (tutorial for both player and developer). |

**Key insight:** The 20-point gap between Obra Dinn (95%) and Gone Home (74%) is largely about interactivity. Both are exploration games. Obra Dinn gives you a TASK (identify the crew). Gone Home gives you exploration. Task-based interaction is the single biggest factor separating "Very Positive" from "Overwhelmingly Positive" in narrative games.

## Addressable Gap: Interactivity

**The biggest risk that's within our control is the "not a game" problem.**

The existing TKA codebase contains interactive systems that could map to museum gameplay:
- Pictograph rendering (decode/translate ancient notations)
- Sequence generation (pattern recognition challenges)
- Retro DOS terminal (already playable)
- Option pickers / construct tools (manipulation interfaces)
- LOOP detection (pattern analysis)
- Browse/library systems (filing cabinet searches)

**Next step:** Audit existing codebase for interactive mechanics that map to museum exhibits. Goal: every phase has at least one interactive element beyond "walk and read."
