# Shared scene-design brief

Approved for consolidation by Austen on 2026-09-05. Apply this to environment
design, substantial art revisions, and visual reviews. Pure technical fixes
need only the affected constraint. Any task lead can use it; no specialist
agent or additional approval process is required.

## Start from the scene's own intent

Read the current scene contract and latest applicable user decisions. State the
place's character, the performer's role, the supported camera space, and what
this pass changes. Keep its palette, density, landmarks, dimensions, and
fantastical logic local. Natural scenes need coherent ecology or geology;
abstract scenes need coherent spatial rhythm, scale, and motion. An intentional
void can be correct. Every scene does not need terrain, trees, or a focal arch.

## Eight design checks

1. **Design the place before furnishing it.** Resolve large forms, enclosure,
   routes, and sightlines before spending on detail. Use a measured plan or
   blockout when those relationships are uncertain. More objects cannot repair
   bad placement, and lighting cannot repair the wrong source silhouette.
2. **Embed the performance area.** Protect the real movement envelope and
   audience access while joining the performance surface to its surroundings.
   Check bodies, props, and effects against the background. Avoid an unexplained
   platform, empty moat, or decorative ring around the origin.
3. **Compose at several scales.** Give foreground, middle distance, and horizon
   distinct jobs. Establish dominant forms, supporting groups, then surface
   detail. Keep useful quiet space. Each object must contribute to the scene;
   uniform scattering and increased object counts do not establish richness.
4. **Make placement explain itself.** Roots meet soil, rocks sit into slopes,
   routes reach destinations, and snow or lava follows the scene's physical
   logic. Vary repeated silhouettes and form purposeful groups. Inspect contacts
   from nearby and after export. Fantasy can depart from physics deliberately.
5. **Make assets share the same conditions.** Reconcile scale, texture density,
   roughness, emission, weathering, and lighting response across sources. Preserve
   material differences inside one environment. Enlarging a sapling does not
   create a mature tree; tinting every object does not establish cohesion.
6. **Use atmosphere to clarify form.** Light, fog, glow, and environmental motion
   should support depth and focal hierarchy. Watch for self-lit organic assets,
   one-color washes, and effects that compete with the performance. Check both
   quiet and active states, including the relevant reduced-motion behavior.
7. **Judge the usable camera space.** Inspect hero, reverse, human-height,
   close-contact, and widest supported views as applicable. Check transitions
   between them. Terrain, water, and sky boundaries must remain intentional.
   Solve exposed edges through coherent world design and the agreed camera
   contract; do not quietly restrict cameras to conceal a weak composition.
8. **Preserve the design through delivery.** Inspect the actual optimized asset
   in its production renderer, with the supported performer counts and effects.
   Quality tiers should reduce detail while retaining defining forms and useful
   visibility. Measure performance against the scene's existing budget. A fast
   empty scene or attractive authoring render cannot prove the delivered result.

## Evidence and ownership

Use [the illustrated examples](../reference/scene-design-examples.md) for concrete
failures and corrections. They preserve historical decisions, not universal
style targets or current runtime certification.

Keep implementation ownership in the [shared 3D contract](../../src/lib/shared/3d/AGENTS.md),
[Blender pipeline](../../.claude/rules/blender-first-3d-scenes.md), and
[canonical capabilities](canonical-capabilities.md). Product controls retain the
[visual canon](visual-design-canon.md). Existing scene or museum gates remain in
force where applicable; this brief adds no new sign-off ceremony.

Compare changed views using the same camera and render settings where possible.
Record the asset/build, camera, viewport, quality, readiness, and what the image
actually demonstrates in the scene's existing evidence record. Open each image.
Passing checks, filenames, and written verdicts cannot replace visual inspection.
Distinguish approved direction, technical verification, and final acceptance.

When a task catches a familiar defect before user review, mention the relevant
check in its normal report. Record actual repeated corrections without claiming
causality from one task. Update shared principles only when a decision transfers
across scenes; keep scene-specific exceptions and creative approvals with their
scene owners. Preserve superseded evidence and its status.
