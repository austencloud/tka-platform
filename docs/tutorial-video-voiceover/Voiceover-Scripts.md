## 1 — The Grid

*Goal: the grid, the three single-hand motions, the six two-hand combinations,
the three positions, a nod to the modes, and the first split-same move.*

Welcome to the Kinetic Alphabet. Everything in this system lives on one grid.

`[CUE: show the grid]`

The grid has four outer points, one center point, and a set of points in
between. Those in-between points are the hand points — the places your hands
travel to, from one beat to the next.

Put one hand on a point. From there you can do exactly three things. You can
**shift** to an adjacent point. You can **dash** straight across to the opposite
point. Or you can stay **static**, right where you are. Shift, dash, static.
That's the entire vocabulary for a single hand.

Now use both hands at once, each doing one of those three things. That gives you
six combinations.

`[CUE: demonstrate each]`

Both hands shift together — dual shifts. This one is huge: it contains all of
VTG, all four modes, and all of quarter time. One hand shifts while the other
stays static. One hand shifts while the other dashes across. One hand dashes
while the other stays static. Both hands dash at once. Or both hands stay static.

Six combinations. Every letter you'll ever learn is built from them.

When both hands land on the grid together, they form a position. There are
three: alpha, beta, and gamma.

**Alpha** means hands apart. One hand east, one hand west — alpha. North and
south — alpha. Turn to face a new direction, still alpha. As long as the hands
are apart, it's alpha.

**Beta** means hands together, stacked on the same point. Wherever they meet,
it's beta.

**Gamma** means a right angle. The hands sit on adjacent points and make an L.
That's gamma.

Alpha, beta, gamma. Apart, together, right angle.

One more thing before we move, and I'll go quick — you get a full lesson on this
later. Motion has modes. Four main ones: split same, together same, together
opposite, and split opposite. Plus two quarter-time modes: quarter-time same
direction, and quarter-time opposite direction. Don't memorize them yet. Just
know they're there.

Here's the payoff. The Type 1 letters cover every way to move between alpha,
beta, and gamma, using all of those modes. That's what this whole series is
about.

Let's put those three positions in motion. Start in alpha, hands apart.

`[CUE: run alpha → alpha, same direction]`

Send both hands the same direction, staying apart, and you land right back in
alpha. Face the camera and it's the same move. Twist the hands, turn the body — as
long as they stay apart, it's still alpha. Alpha to alpha.

`[CUE: run alpha → beta]`

Now bring the hands toward each other instead, until they meet and stack on a
single point. That's a move into beta.

`[CUE: run alpha → gamma]`

And bring them to a right angle — one hand square to the other, making that L.
That's a move into gamma.

Alpha, beta, gamma. Apart, together, right angle — and your shifts, dashes, and
statics are what carry you between them. Every letter in this series is just a
named route from one position to another. That's the whole alphabet.

That's your grid, your motions, and your positions. Next video, we put a staff in
your hand.

## 2 — Positions

*Goal: the staff on the grid, radial, the two prerequisites, negative space, the
orientation rule (iso keeps, anti swaps), reading a pictograph, positions with
two staves.*

Now let's put a staff on the grid.

Your hand goes on a hand point, and the staff radiates out from the center. One
end sits near the middle of the grid — right around your solar plexus — and the
other end points out to the edge. That's a radial staff.

Before you touch the letters, you need two skills. Just two.

First, the **isolation** — thumb lead or pinky lead, either one.

`[CUE: isolation]`

Second, the **four-petal anti-spin**, and you need to take it through negative
space.

`[CUE: four-petal anti-spin]`

Negative space is the room above your shoulder, right here. Start thumb-in and
that's the pocket you pass through, up top. Start thumb-out and the negative
space shows up down here instead — not in front, but low and behind. Learn to
find both. Almost every pattern in the Kinetic Alphabet depends on it.

Here's the single most useful rule for reading your own movement. It's about
orientation — which way your thumb points.

In an isolation, the thumb orientation never changes. Start thumb-in, stay
thumb-in, the whole way through. Thumb in, thumb in, thumb in, thumb in.

In an anti-spin, the logic flips — the orientation swaps. Start thumb-in, do one
anti-spin, and now you're thumb-out. Follow through, thumb-in again. Out, in,
out, in.

So: isolations keep their orientation, anti-spins swap it. Hold onto that. It's
what lets you glance at a pictograph, check the orientation it shows, and know
instantly whether you've been running the sequence right.

Now pick up both staves and let's find the three positions again.

Hands in **alpha**: the staves sit in a line, each one radial, radiating out from
the center. Vertical alpha, horizontal alpha, vertical again.

Hands in **beta**: the staves stack on one hand point, both radiating out. They
act like a single staff.

Hands in **gamma**: the hands sit at a right angle and make that L shape.

Alpha, beta, gamma — now with staves in your hands. Next, our first letter: A.

## 3 — Letter A

*Goal: split-same isolations = A, the twist, the body-turn solution, both sides,
discrete vs continuous.*

This is the letter A.

Start in a vertical alpha. Right hand on top, both thumbs in. Not thumbs up — some
people start there, but that's not us. Thumbs in.

`[CUE: starting position]`

For A, both hands isolate at the same time, and you land in a horizontal alpha. Do
it again — both hands isolate — and you're back to vertical alpha. That's A.

Keep going, though, and you hit a wall. One more time and you're twisted up. You
can't continue.

`[CUE: show the twist]`

So rewind to right here. To get through the twist and keep the motion alive, take
one hand — the right, in this case — and bring it behind your body. As you keep
isolating, right hand down, left hand up, the right comes up behind you and your
upper body turns to face the side. From there the right keeps isolating up, the
left comes down, and even though you're facing sideways now, one gentle turn of
the body brings you home.

Let's put it together. A. Turn. A. And turn back home. A.

A little faster: A, A, A, A.

You can also send the staves the same direction while turning your body the other
way. It feels slightly different, but it's the same letter. Start the same. A —
now I prepare to turn to my left as the left staff rises and the right drops. A,
A, A. The left staff drops behind me, I keep twisting to hold the isolation, the
right comes back up, the left comes down, and one turn to face front brings me
home.

So there's A on this side — A, A, A, A — and A on the other side — A, A, A, A.

And you don't have to run it continuously. One A, reset, another A. Continuous or
one at a time, it's the same thing.

These are split-same isolations. One unit of split-same isolations is the letter
A.

## 4 — Letter B (front facing)

*Goal: B = two anti-spins on the A hand path, the negative-space pockets, the
elbow trick out of the twist, the above/below sneak and the body-turn advantage.*

B follows the exact same hand path as A. The only change: two anti-spins instead
of two isolations.

Start the same way — right hand on top, thumbs in, alpha.

For B you'll live in that negative space you practiced. The right thumb-end passes
through the pocket at the shoulder while the left anti-spins up. That's B.

`[CUE: beat one]`

Follow through. Now the left anti-spins up through negative space, and the right
anti-spins down. B.

Keep twisting to see what happens. The right anti-spins up, the left anti-spins
down, and your arms cross. B. Now it looks like you're stuck, the same way A got
stuck. You're not — and here's the trick.

Find the pinky-end of your left staff — this one. Bring it right in front of your
nose so you know you've got the right end. Now make eye contact with your right
elbow. Take that pinky-end and bring it behind the elbow. You can barely see it
from out front. Pull it in toward you, trace it down along your armpit, and you
can anti-spin both staves right back into place.

Let's run it a couple more times. Right hand through negative space. Left up
through negative space. Follow through and cross — your left ends on top of your
right. To clear the twist, find that left pinky-end, bring it behind the inner
elbow, trace down the armpit, follow all the way through.

One more. Right through negative space. Left through negative space. Cross the
arms. Pinky-end behind the inner elbow, and follow through to the end.

There's a second way to handle it. Start the same. One, two. But instead of
bringing the right hand under the left on the next move, sneak the right staff
*above* the arm — right through there. Because you fixed the problem early, beat
four takes care of itself. Just anti-spin both back to right-hand-on-top.

Let's drill it. One, two — right hand sneaks above the left arm as the left
anti-spins down — three, four. Again: one, two, sneak above, three, four. One
more: one, two, above, three.

So that's B, facing your audience, with two options — sneak above, or sneak below.
And the direction you sneak changes how you want to turn. Going above, it's
cleaner to turn your body back this way than to force the ends stacked toward the
audience. Going below — the first version we worked — it's cleaner to turn the
other way. Practice both. Above, and below.

## 5 — Letter B (body turns)

*Goal: turning the body through B, with negative space, on both sides, then
alternating both sides with counts.*

You've done B with negative space. Now let's add a body turn — and then do both at
once. This gets complex, so move with me slowly.

Turn your body to the right. Start the motion: right staff through negative space
as the left anti-spins up. Facing this way now, bring the left up through negative
space as the right comes down. Follow through — left forward, right back. Then the
right rises up through negative space again.

Let's take that slower, because it's a lot. Left up with an anti-spin, right down
with an anti-spin. As the left rises, carry it through negative space — vertical.
Left down, right up. As the right rises, carry it through negative space —
vertical again. Left up, right down, through negative space. Left down, right up,
through negative space.

One more, less talking. One. Two. Three. Four — negative space every time.

Now the other way. Turn your body to the left, and keep using negative space as
much as you can.

The right goes through negative space as the left comes up. This next part sounds
strange, so trust me: the left pinky-end, the one closest to the floor, has to
come up behind you on this side. That's just for this variation. Then the right
comes down and the left comes up, ready to pass through the negative space — right
there, just about to pass.

Same thing on this side. Left through negative space, right anti-spins up. Keep
that pinky-end on the outside of the arm — closer to the camera. Follow through:
right up, left down, ready to bring the right through negative space.

A few more. Right through negative space, continue. Left through negative space,
continue. Right, continue. Left, continue. Nice.

Now let's alternate sides, the way we did with A. Slowly, both sides.

Right hand through negative space as it comes down — one. Left up through negative
space — two. Right rises up behind you, left comes forward — three. Right up
through negative space, back home — four.

Continue into the second half. Right through negative space as the left comes up,
and bring that left pinky-end behind your elbow. Left up, ready for negative
space, right down. Left through negative space, right up. Right up ready for
negative space, left down.

Both sides together now. Right through negative space — one. Left up through
negative space — two. Turn to the right — three. Right through negative space —
four. Watch the wrist as you go.

Then pick up the pace: one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight. Faster.
That's B on both sides, turning, through negative space.

## 6 — Letter C

*Goal: recap A/B, C = hybrid (iso + anti), twice the variations, pick a
convention, the elbow trick, the early sneak-under, reversing, turning with C on
both sides.*

Quick recap. With A, we isolated both hands and found the twist. With B, we
learned to get through that twist by sneaking around the elbow — there are more
ways than the one we covered, but you've got one. And we learned to turn with B,
the same way we turned with A. Lots to track between negative space and turns, but
commit to your path and you get through it.

Now, C. C is the hybrid: one hand isolates, one hand anti-spins. Same hand path as
A and B. Because the two hands are doing different things, C has twice as many
variations.

Let's pick one and keep it consistent for the whole video. Right hand on top,
thumbs in. Right hand isolates, left hand anti-spins, hands moving clockwise.
That's C.

Follow through: left up through negative space while the right isolates down — C
again. Anti-spin the left down, isolate the right up, cross your arms. And here's
where B pays off — you already know this twist. Find the left pinky-end, bring it
behind the inner elbow of the right arm, trace the body, anti-spin down, and
isolate the right up.

Run it. Reset: thumbs in, right on top. Left anti up, right iso down — one. Left
up through negative space — two. Cross the arms — three. Pinky-end down on the
inside — four. Again: one, two, three, four.

Same early fix as B. Start one, two — then, instead of passing the anti-spinning
hand over the top, sneak its thumb-end under the elbow one beat early — three —
and four takes care of itself. One, two, sneak under, three, four.

Keeping our convention — right isolates, left anti-spins — you can run C every
direction. Reverse it. Go under instead of over. Or switch which hand does which.
That's your homework: explore all of it, on both sides.

Now let's turn with C, exactly like A and B. Right hand iso, left hand anti. Turn
to the right first, then the left.

Right isolates down, left anti-spins up — one. Left anti-spins up through negative
space as the right isolates down, facing right — two. Right isolates up behind you
as your body turns right, left anti-spins forward — three. Right isolates up, left
anti-spins down, back to thumbs in, right on top — four. Again, no words: one,
two, three, four.

Other side now. The left hand feels like the work we did in B; the right hand
feels like A. Coming up on one, keep that pinky-end on the outside of the elbow.
Left anti up, right iso down — thumbs in. Left through negative space, right up,
left down, home. Watch your thumbs as you go: in, mixed, in, mixed, in.

Now alternate sides, back and forth. Right iso down, left anti up — one. Left up
through negative space — two. Right up behind, left forward — three. Follow
through — four. Other side: one — turn that left so the pinky-end is outside —
two, left through negative space, three, four. Then run it up to speed: one
through eight, both sides.

## 7 — Wrap Up

*Goal: A/B/C = split-same pattern, the iso/anti/hybrid triple repeats through
Type 1, the J-K-L inference trick, the sign-off.*

Let's tie it together. A, B, and C make up the split-same hand path, and they
follow a pattern. A is two isolations. B is two anti-spins. C is the hybrid — one
hand isolating, one anti-spinning. Iso-iso, anti-anti, hybrid.

That pattern repeats, and it's how you memorize the whole alphabet.

Say someone shows you a motion and tells you it's K. K is two anti-spins. From
that one fact you can fill in its neighbors. The letter before it, J, has to be
two isolations. The letter after it, L, has to be the hybrid. Iso, anti, hybrid —
J, K, L.

That triple — two isolations, then two anti-spins, then the hybrid — runs all the
way through the Type 1 letters. So from a single piece of information, you can
work out the letters next to it.

That's the first series. I hope it was useful. I'd genuinely love your feedback —
tell me what landed and what I could do better. See you in the next one.

# Part II — Compound Letters (LOOPs)

Rewritten from the DJ, EK, and FL tutorials. Longer, more advanced videos — same
treatment: your voice, filler and endless count-runs trimmed, every teaching beat
kept.

**The idea:** in continuous spinning you don't run single letters, you run
**compounds** — two half-cycles that close into a loop. A letter that goes beta to
alpha, paired with its partner that goes alpha to back to beta, makes one
repeating cycle. D+J, E+K, F+L. And they inherit the A-B-C pattern: **DJ is the
isolation loop, EK is the anti-spin loop, FL is the hybrid.** (Canon has cute
mnemonics for these — Disco Jam, Exploding Kitten, Fruity Loops — if you want a
memory hook.)

Note: EK is one loop taught across two videos. "Tog-opp" and "split-opp" aren't
different moves — it's the same EK cycle entered from two vantages (the beta-phase
start reads as tog-opp, the side-beta start reads as split-opp).

## 8 — DJ

*Goal: the alpha/beta/gamma map, compounds, DJ = open-D / close-J isolations,
crossing arms both sides, body turns, the split-opposite vantage, the 12-beat
structure, thumbs-out.*

Take a look at the main page of the alphabet. A, B, C, D, E, F all end in alpha.
G, H, I, J, K, L all end in beta. M through V all end in gamma. So the moment you
see where a motion ends, you've narrowed it down. Ends in alpha, it's one of the
first six. Ends in beta, it's G through L. Ends in gamma, it's M through V.

We've already done A, B, and C — the three that go alpha to alpha. Now meet their
neighbors. D, E, and F start in beta and end in alpha. J, K, and L start in alpha
and end in beta.

Here's why that matters. A, B, C repeat cleanly — do an A, follow the same hand
path, and you get another A, and another. D, E, F don't loop on their own, because
they run the opposite hand path. But pair a beta-to-alpha letter with an
alpha-to-beta letter and you get a complete cycle: beta to alpha, back to beta.
That's a compound. D plus J. E plus K. F plus L.

And they inherit the A-B-C pattern. DJ is the isolation loop. EK is the anti-spin
loop. FL is the hybrid. This video is DJ — isolations — and a few ways to run it.

`[CUE: start thumbs in, low beta]`

We'll start thumbs in, though thumbs out, or one in and one out, is just as valid.
From this low beta, isolate both hands up and open into alpha. That's D. Follow
through from alpha back to beta — that's J. Open D, close J. Reverse it: open D,
close J. Again — open D, close J.

`[CUE: crossing arms, right in front]`

Now let's keep it going by crossing the hands. Pick one hand to travel in front —
say the right, so we stay consistent. Right hand travels in front, left stays
closer to the body, cross, and do D. Follow through into beta for J. D, J. Pass the
right in front. D, J. You'll feel a natural lean back to your right every time the
right passes in front.

`[CUE: left in front]`

Now the left in front. Left travels further out, right comes closer. D, J. This
time you lean back to your left. D, J.

`[CUE: alternate sides]`

Now alternate — right in front, then left in front, from that low beta. D, J,
right in front. D, J, left in front. A couple more, right, then left. That's DJ
facing the audience, crossing arms.

`[CUE: body turns, right]`

Same as A, B, and C, we can turn the body. From low beta, aim to turn right. It
starts the same — D, J — but by J you've turned to face the right. Now the right
hand passes behind you, toward the curtain, and the left comes in front, both
isolating. D, J. And you're home.

`[CUE: body turns, left]`

Other side. Start the same, then turn left through the J. The left hand comes down
behind you, the right comes in front. D, J. Home.

`[CUE: alternate turns, four times]`

Now alternate — right, then left, four times. D, J, turn right, home. D, J, turn
left, home. Keep going.

`[CUE: split-opposite vantage, side beta]`

Everything so far is the together-opposite hand path — the one some people call
air. You can also run DJ from a side beta, and now you're in the split-opposite
hand path. From the side beta, thumbs in, right hand down, left hand up. D — thumbs
still in — J. Keep it flowing. D, J. Start from the other side if you like: right
up, left down. D, J.

You can turn with the split-opposite version too. Start turned toward the right,
right down, left up, then twist your body toward the back — a big twist, all the
way over — and follow through to the other side. D, J. Keep the direction going and
it carries you all the way around.

`[CUE: full run with count]`

Now the whole thing, less talking. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight,
nine, ten, eleven, twelve.

Why twelve? Four beats facing front, four through the turns one way, four the
other. Four and four and four is twelve.

`[CUE: thumbs out]`

And you can run all of it thumbs out instead. Same shape, different feel — now the
pinky ends stay into the center the whole time. D, J. D, J. And with turns. D, J.

## 9 — EK — Together-Opposite

*Goal: E matches K like D matched J but through anti-spins; open-E to alpha,
close-K to beta through negative space; continuous with the crossing
negative-space pass-throughs on both sides; alternating; body turns using the
outside-of-body variation.*

This is EK — the anti-spin loop. E matches with K exactly the way D matched with J.
E travels from beta to alpha through two anti-spins. K travels from alpha to beta
through two anti-spins. E, K.

`[CUE: low beta, thumbs in]`

Start in low beta, thumbs in. For E, anti-spin both hands up and open into alpha.
The thumb ends that were pointing in are now pointing out. To follow through to
beta at the top, carry those thumb ends through the negative space above your
shoulders — right through. That's K.

Reverse it — back through that same negative space into alpha, follow through,
thumb ends to the center, pinky ends down, home in beta. Open E, close K. Again: E,
K. Reverse, E, K.

`[CUE: continuous, right in front]`

Now continuously. Hands together in beta, open E, follow through K. To keep going,
pick a hand in front — the right. The right passes in front of everything; the left
passes behind the right arm, through its negative space. E — now you're thumb-out,
arms crossed, leaning back to your right. Follow through: pinky ends down, thumb
ends to center, and the right staff travels underneath the left arm's negative
space — watch it pass right behind — into beta.

Run it a couple times. Beta, open E, close K, right further out, left closer, left
pinky-end through the right arm's negative space. E — right staff under the left
arm — K.

`[CUE: left in front]`

Now the left in front. Same thing, other side. E, K. Left further out, pass in
front, right staff through the left arm's negative space. Lean back to your left —
it's easier. The left staff travels under the right arm for K.

`[CUE: alternate sides]`

Alternate — right in front, then left. E, K, right, lean right. E, K, left, lean
left. A few more.

`[CUE: body turns, outside-of-body variation]`

Now add body turns, and this changes things. Prepare to turn right. Open E, turn to
the right — and here's the difference. Turning this way, we drop the negative space
and use a different variation: the thumb ends pass on the outside of the body, not
the inside. Coming up for E, both thumb ends ride outside the arms. Then the right
staff anti-spins down behind you, the left anti-spins forward. E. Follow through,
thumb ends to center, pinky ends straight down. K. Home.

Work that side: E, turn right, K, thumbs on the outside, follow through. E, K.

`[CUE: left side]`

Same on the left. Prepare to turn left. E, turn left, both staves pass on the
outside of the arms, anti-spin down for K. Home. A few more. E, K.

`[CUE: alternate turns]`

Now alternate — face right, then face left. E, turn right, K, home. E, turn left,
K, home. Both sides, a few times through. Nice work.

That's EK from the together-opposite vantage. Next video, the same loop from the
split-opposite side.

## 10 — EK — Split-Opposite

*Goal: EK from the side beta; the important awkward pass-through position; thumbs-in
then thumbs-out; the 90-degree turn; mixed orientation; side beta plus body turns
(the super-twist); outro into FL.*

You've done EK from the low beta. Now we start from a side beta, and it opens up a
whole new set of considerations. Same anti-spins, different orientation, different
feel.

`[CUE: side beta, thumbs in]`

Side beta, thumbs in — and we'll keep it consistent with DJ. Right hand comes down,
left hand comes up. Both staves start in toward the center, between your arms. The
left anti-spins up, the right anti-spins down, and the right lands in a position
that feels awkward at first. Learn it — you pass through it constantly. Thumbs are
out, the left thumb points up, the right thumb points down, pinky ends in the
center. Now push those pinky ends to the left and bring the thumb ends toward the
center as you anti-spin again. E — and you're in beta on this side.

Follow through: right hand up, left hand down. E. Watch that odd hand position,
it's intentional. Follow through, pinky ends travel right, thumb ends to center, K.
Home.

Run it faster a few times. Right down, left up, E, follow through K. Right up, left
down, E, follow through K. Thumbs out, thumbs in. E, K.

`[CUE: thumbs-out start]`

We've been starting thumbs in. Start thumbs out instead and the shape looks
identical but feels completely different. Twist your wrists so the pinky ends sit on
the outside of the body. Thumb ends move to the center, pinky ends straight down.
E. Now the thumb ends are in, pinky ends out. Turn your body ninety degrees to the
left, thumb ends traveling left, pinky ends to the center — and keep those pinky
ends on the outside of the elbow, not the inside. Follow through: right up with an
anti-spin, left down. E, thumbs in, turn to the right, thumb ends push right, pinky
ends down and onto the outside of the elbows, home. A few more. E, K.

`[CUE: mixed orientation]`

Push it further — one thumb in, one thumb out. Start left thumb out, right thumb
in, and run EK from that mixed position, borrowing from both versions. Right down,
left up. E — right thumb out, left thumb in, both pointing at the floor. Follow
through, K — right in, left out, left pinky-end outside the elbow. Follow through,
right up, left down. E — both thumb ends pointing up now. K — and home. Thumbs down,
thumbs mixed, thumbs up, thumbs mixed. The left-in, right-out version can be your
homework.

`[CUE: side beta plus body turns]`

Now the hard one — side beta with body turns, like we did with DJ. Thumbs in, left
up, right down. E. Now, instead of turning back to your audience, keep turning left
and face away for E — thumbs out — then twist all the way around to the far side for
K, fully twisted, thumbs in. Follow through, right down, left up, E, and keep
twisting. Then carry it all the way to the right — a maximal twist, you'll have to
step your feet to get there — E, thumbs out, K. Follow through, right up, left down,
facing back now, K, home.

Whole thing, less talking. E, K, twist left. E, K. Right down, left up. E, K. Four
more, twist far right. E, K. E, K.

`[CUE: outro]`

From here you can start thumbs out, or thumbs mixed, and turn — each opens its own
bag of negative-space problems. I'll leave those to you, so this doesn't run
forever. Happy practicing. Next one, we do FL.

## 11 — FL

*Goal: FL = the hybrid compound (cousin of C); F+L on the same hand paths as DJ and
EK; low-beta open-F / close-L with the crossing on both role assignments, body
turns, the split-opposite side beta (flamenco grip, thumbs in/mixed), side-beta
turns + the 12-count, outro.*

Last compound: FL — the hybrid loop. F and L run the same hand paths as DJ and EK,
but now the two hands do different things. F travels beta to alpha, L travels alpha
to beta. And because it's the hybrid, one hand isolates while the other anti-spins.
It's the loop cousin of the letter C.

`[CUE: main page — highlight F and L]`

For this whole video, keep one convention: the right hand isolates, the left hand
anti-spins. Everything here also works with the roles swapped — that's your
homework.

`[CUE: low beta, thumbs in]`

Start in low beta, thumbs in, and follow the same path as DJ and EK. Open: the
right isolates up, the left anti-spins up — that's F, and you're in alpha. Close:
bring the left up through the negative space above your shoulder — that's L, back to
beta. Open F, close L. Reverse it — left back through the negative space, right
isolates down. F, L.

`[CUE: continuous, cross — right in front]`

Now go continuous, all the way around. Open F, close L, and here — just like C —
you get to pick how to proceed. Take the isolating hand, the right, and pass it in
front; the left goes behind, thumb-end inside the shoulder. The left travels through
the right shoulder's negative space as the right isolates down, then the left
pinky-end leads in front and comes down for L. F, L. F, L.

`[CUE: swap the leading hand]`

Now swap which hand leads in front — the left isolating hand travels in front, the
right anti-spinning hand passes through the left shoulder's negative space. F, L.
You'll flip between the two versions at the low point: right-iso, then left-iso,
back and forth. F, L, F, L.

`[CUE: body turns]`

Now turn the body and travel all the way around. Open F, turn to the right — the
left comes up on the outside of the body instead of through negative space, and that
sends you into the turn. L. The right drops behind you with an isolation, the left
comes in front. F. Both come down to low beta. L. Home. Run it the other way,
turning left, roles mirrored. F, L.

`[CUE: alternate turns, both assignments]`

Do it turning right, then turning left. Then swap the iso and anti hands and run
both sides again. F, L, F, L. That evens out the whole turning section.

`[CUE: side beta — split-opposite]`

Now, just like DJ and EK, start from a side beta instead of low beta — same letters,
F and L, the split-opposite feel. If you haven't done the EK split-opposite video,
go do that one first; this builds on it. Right hand up, left hand down. The left
anti-spins down into a flamenco grip, the right isolates up, and you're in a
vertical alpha. F. Follow through side to side, right down, left up, back to side
beta. L. Watch the thumbs: in on beta, mixed on F, in again on L. F, L. Then reverse
the roles — left isolates, right anti-spins — and run it from the left. F, L.

`[CUE: side beta with turns + count]`

Last section: side beta with body turns. It's a twelve-count — four here, four
through the turn, four back — same shape as DJ. Start side beta, right iso, left
anti. F, thumbs mixed. Keep twisting the body left as you come up behind. L. Twist
further, all the way around. F. Follow through. L. It carries you the whole way
around. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve.
Run it in each role assignment.

`[CUE: outro]`

That's FL. And that's the three compound loops: DJ the isolation loop, EK the
anti-spin loop, FL the hybrid — the same iso / anti / hybrid pattern as A, B, and C,
one level up. The rest of the role-and-direction variations are yours to explore.
Thanks for following along, and sorry this one took a while. See you in the next
one.
