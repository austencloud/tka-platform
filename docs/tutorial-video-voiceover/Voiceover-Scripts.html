The Kinetic Alphabet

Eleven scripts, rewritten from the audio of the originals — the seven-part first series, plus the DJ, EK, and FL compounds. Same teaching, same terminology, your voice; filler cut, beat counts kept.

How to use this

  • Read it, don't perform the brackets. Everything in serif is spoken. The red CUE blocks are stage directions for you, never read aloud.
  • Works two ways. Voice it over the existing clips (order and beats match the originals) or shoot new footage and follow the same beats.
  • Beat counts stay. The "one, two, three, four" walk-throughs are load-bearing for a movement tutorial. Rambles, false starts, and repeated count-throughs are gone.
  • Fixes from the transcript: stabs → staves four-pedal → four-petal C: "pro and anti" → one hand isolates, one anti-spins
  • Checked against the knowledge base. The memorization trick holds: every Type 1 group runs iso-iso / anti-anti / hybrid, so A-B-C and J-K-L share the same shape.

The Grid

3:45 → ~3:00

The grid, the three single-hand motions, the six two-hand combinations, the three positions, a nod to the modes, and the first split-same move.

Welcome to the Kinetic Alphabet. Everything in this system lives on one grid.

CUE Show the grid.

The grid has four outer points, one center point, and a set of points in between. Those in-between points are the hand points — the places your hands travel to, from one beat to the next.

Put one hand on a point. From there you can do exactly three things. You can shift to an adjacent point. You can dash straight across to the opposite point. Or you can stay static, right where you are. Shift, dash, static. That's the entire vocabulary for a single hand.

Now use both hands at once, each doing one of those three things. That gives you six combinations.

CUE Demonstrate each combination.

Both hands shift together — dual shifts. This one is huge: it contains all of VTG, all four modes, and all of quarter time. One hand shifts while the other stays static. One hand shifts while the other dashes across. One hand dashes while the other stays static. Both hands dash at once. Or both hands stay static.

Six combinations. Every letter you'll ever learn is built from them.

When both hands land on the grid together, they form a position. There are three: alpha, beta, and gamma.

Alpha means hands apart. One hand east, one hand west — alpha. North and south — alpha. Turn to face a new direction, still alpha. As long as the hands are apart, it's alpha.

Beta means hands together, stacked on the same point. Wherever they meet, it's beta.

Gamma means a right angle. The hands sit on adjacent points and make an L. That's gamma.

Alpha, beta, gamma. Apart, together, right angle.

One more thing before we move, and I'll go quick — you get a full lesson on this later. Motion has modes. Four main ones: split same, together same, together opposite, and split opposite. Plus two quarter-time modes: quarter-time same direction, and quarter-time opposite direction. Don't memorize them yet. Just know they're there.

Here's the payoff. The Type 1 letters cover every way to move between alpha, beta, and gamma, using all of those modes. That's what this whole series is about.

Let's put those three positions in motion. Start in alpha, hands apart.

CUE Run alpha → alpha, same direction.

Send both hands the same direction, staying apart, and you land right back in alpha. Face the camera and it's the same move. Twist the hands, turn the body — as long as they stay apart, it's still alpha. Alpha to alpha.

CUE Run alpha → beta.

Now bring the hands toward each other instead, until they meet and stack on a single point. That's a move into beta.

CUE Run alpha → gamma.

And bring them to a right angle — one hand square to the other, making that L. That's a move into gamma.

Alpha, beta, gamma. Apart, together, right angle — and your shifts, dashes, and statics are what carry you between them. Every letter in this series is just a named route from one position to another. That's the whole alphabet.

That's your grid, your motions, and your positions. Next video, we put a staff in your hand.

Positions

2:49 → ~2:30

The staff on the grid, radial, the two prerequisites, negative space, the orientation rule (iso keeps, anti swaps), reading a pictograph, positions with two staves.

Now let's put a staff on the grid.

Your hand goes on a hand point, and the staff radiates out from the center. One end sits near the middle of the grid — right around your solar plexus — and the other end points out to the edge. That's a radial staff.

Before you touch the letters, you need two skills. Just two.

First, the isolation — thumb lead or pinky lead, either one.

CUE Isolation.

Second, the four-petal anti-spin, and you need to take it through negative space.

CUE Four-petal anti-spin.

Negative space is the room above your shoulder, right here. Start thumb-in and that's the pocket you pass through, up top. Start thumb-out and the negative space shows up down here instead — not in front, but low and behind. Learn to find both. Almost every pattern in the Kinetic Alphabet depends on it.

Here's the single most useful rule for reading your own movement. It's about orientation — which way your thumb points.

In an isolation, the thumb orientation never changes. Start thumb-in, stay thumb-in, the whole way through. Thumb in, thumb in, thumb in, thumb in.

In an anti-spin, the logic flips — the orientation swaps. Start thumb-in, do one anti-spin, and now you're thumb-out. Follow through, thumb-in again. Out, in, out, in.

So: isolations keep their orientation, anti-spins swap it. Hold onto that. It's what lets you glance at a pictograph, check the orientation it shows, and know instantly whether you've been running the sequence right.

Now pick up both staves and let's find the three positions again.

Hands in alpha: the staves sit in a line, each one radial, radiating out from the center. Vertical alpha, horizontal alpha, vertical again.

Hands in beta: the staves stack on one hand point, both radiating out. They act like a single staff.

Hands in gamma: the hands sit at a right angle and make that L shape.

Alpha, beta, gamma — now with staves in your hands. Next, our first letter: A.

Wrap Up

A/B/C = split-same pattern, the iso/anti/hybrid triple repeats through Type 1, the J-K-L inference trick, the sign-off.

Let's tie it together. A, B, and C make up the split-same hand path, and they follow a pattern. A is two isolations. B is two anti-spins. C is the hybrid — one hand isolating, one anti-spinning. Iso-iso, anti-anti, hybrid.

That pattern repeats, and it's how you memorize the whole alphabet.

That triple — two isolations, then two anti-spins, then the hybrid — runs all the way through the Type 1 letters. So from a single piece of information, you can work out the letters next to it.

Part II

Compound Letters — LOOPs

In continuous spinning you don't run single letters, you run compounds — two half-cycles that close into a loop. A letter that goes beta to alpha, paired with its partner that goes alpha back to beta, makes one repeating cycle. They inherit the A-B-C pattern.

DJ = isolation loop · cousin of A EK = anti-spin loop · cousin of B FL = hybrid · cousin of C

EK is one loop taught across two videos. "Tog-opp" and "split-opp" aren't different moves — it's the same EK cycle entered from two vantages: the beta-phase start reads as together-opposite, the side-beta start reads as split-opposite.
