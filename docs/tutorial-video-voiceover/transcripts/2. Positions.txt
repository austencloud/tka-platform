# 2. Positions

## Plain
To put the staff on our grid, we place our hand at one of the hand points. Let's choose the bottom hand point. The staff, the line that the staff makes, is going to radiate out from the center point. So if I'm holding my staff in the center, one end of the staff is going to be at the center point of the grid, which is usually around the solar plexus, and the other one is going to be at the outer edge of the grid. There are two prerequisites to learning every letter in the kinetic alphabet. One is to be able to do an isolation like this, either thumb lead or pinky lead. The other one is to be able to do a four-pedal anti-spin like this, with the ability to use negative space. And that's the space above my shoulder right here. That's what I encounter when I start with a thumb in. If I start with a thumb out, instead I'll encounter a negative space down here. In the front I don't get it, but down here I go through negative space. Those are essential parts of being able to do all sorts of patterns in the kinetic alphabet. It's important to understand that when you do an isolation, the thumb orientation starts in and ends in. In other words, the thumb orientation stays the same over the course of the entire motion. My thumb is in, my thumb is in, my thumb is in, my thumb is in. The logic is reversed for anti-spins. So with an anti-spin, if you start thumb in and you do a single anti-spin, the orientation is now going to be thumb out. If I follow through, that thumb out orientation turns into a thumb in orientation. Following through, it's thumb out, and following through, it's thumb in. So it's very important that you understand isolations keep their orientation, and anti-spins swap their orientation. This is useful because it allows you to look at a pictograph which contains the orientation and determine if you've been doing the sequence correct based on the orientation you're currently looking at. If we pick up our two stabs now, to put our hands in alpha, we would have both our stabs in a line, like so. And each staff is in a radial position, so it's radiating out from the center. This would be a vertical alpha, horizontal, vertical. If my hands are in beta, the stabs are on top of each other at one of these hand points, radiating out from the center. They're acting as though they're one staff. And if they're in gamma, our hands will be at a right angle, forming that shape.
