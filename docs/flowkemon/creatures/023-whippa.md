# #023 — Whippa

> A crack splits the air. Light streaks past your face. Something just moved faster than you could see.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Name | Whippa |
| Prop | Pixel Whip / Fiber Optic Whip |
| Tier | 4 — Legendary |
| Rarity | Spawns during storms. Moves too fast to track. You hear it before you see it. |
| Animal Analog | Cheetah — explosive speed, light on its feet, hunts in bursts |

## Appearance

A handle — compact, cylindrical, dark — from which hundreds of thin fiber optic strands extend, each tipped with a point of colored light. At rest, the fibers drape like hair, pooling on the ground in a luminous puddle. In motion, they fan into a single whip-body that cracks through the air as a unified streak of color.

The fibers have individual physics. They separate during wide swings, merge during fast cracks, tangle briefly and untangle, and settle differently every time. No two resting poses are identical. The handle has a single LED array at the base that drives color up through each fiber — the color propagates from base to tip with a visible travel time, so fast movements leave gradient trails.

The light is sharp, not diffuse. Where Glowr is ambient bioluminescence, Whippa is a laser show. Clean lines, high contrast, light that cuts rather than glows.

No googly eyes. The handle's LED ring serves as the "eye" — a focused point of bright white that stares directly at whatever Whippa is tracking.

## Movement & Behavior

### Idle
- The fibers drape and pool. Whippa is still. Conserving.
- Occasionally: a single fiber lifts from the pool and sways, testing the air. Like a cat's tail twitching before a pounce.
- Then: nothing. Back to stillness. The pounce isn't yet.
- Rare idle event (every 45-60 seconds): Whippa cracks once. Full extension, instant retraction. The light trail hangs in the air for 1.5 seconds after the fibers have already returned to rest. The speed is startling every time.

### Alert (player approaches)
- The fiber pool lifts slightly. All strands hover an inch off the ground. Tension.
- The handle rotates to face the player. The LED ring brightens.
- If the player moves slowly: Whippa tracks. The fibers angle toward the player like compass needles.
- If the player moves suddenly: Whippa cracks. Not at the player — past them. A warning shot. The light trail passes within a meter of the player's model. The audio crack is instantaneous. The message is clear: I'm faster.

### Happy (after capture / in party)
- Fibers flow loosely, trailing behind Whippa as it moves alongside the player. Relaxed.
- Performs "flow" patterns — figure-8s, jellyfish, behind-the-back wraps — the fiber whip manipulation vocabulary, executed at comfortable speed
- Chases small particle effects and environmental objects (falling leaves, insects), cracking at them playfully. Rarely hits them. Doesn't need to. The pursuit is the game.
- Occasionally wraps a loose fiber around the player's arm briefly, then releases. A nudge. Come on, let's go.

### Startled
- Every fiber stands on end. Full extension in all directions simultaneously — a sea urchin of light.
- Holds for a fraction of a second.
- Then: retraction to a tight coil around the handle. Everything pulled in. Compact. The smallest possible target.
- From the coil: a rapid series of cracks in a 360-degree pattern. Whippa can't see the threat, so it's striking everywhere. The light trails create a momentary cage of streaks around it.

### Sleep
- Handle rests on the ground, upright.
- Fibers draped in a perfect circle around it, tips outward. Like a sleeping cat's body curled around its head.
- Occasional fiber twitch. A single strand lifts, curls, resettles.
- The LED color cycles very slowly through warm tones. Amber. Red. Amber. A nightlight, not a light show.

## How You Encounter It

Whippa appears during storm weather events — rain, wind, electrical storms. The atmospheric discharge gives it energy. Lightning is fast; Whippa is faster.

Prerequisite: the player must have successfully caught at least one creature on their first attempt (no re-tries). Whippa respects clean execution. Sloppy players who brute-force captures through repetition won't trigger its spawn.

The encounter begins with sound. A crack. Unmistakable. Not thunder — too sharp, too short, too close. Then a light trail, visible for just over a second, slashing across the player's field of view. Not in the distance. Right here.

Whippa is circling the player. Fast. The player can't track it. They see afterimages: a streak of green there, magenta there, white cutting through rain. Each crack comes from a different direction. Whippa is everywhere and nowhere.

The player has to stop and wait. Like Glowr, movement won't work. But unlike Glowr's patient observation, Whippa's test is nerve. The cracks get closer. The light trails pass nearer. The player stands in the center of a tightening spiral of speed and sound and light. Don't flinch.

When the spiral tightens to within arm's reach and the player hasn't moved, Whippa stops. In front of them. Fibers pooled. Handle upright. LED ring locked on the player's position. First time the player sees the creature as a creature rather than a blur.

The stillness is the hardest part. Whippa just showed you it can hit anything. Standing still while it spirals closer takes trust in the encounter design. Flinch — take a single step — and Whippa vanishes. Re-triggers next storm. Same test.

## Capture Sequence Difficulty

Speed. Pure speed.

Whippa's capture sequence has the shortest timing windows of any creature. Beats arrive in rapid bursts — not constant fast tempo, but explosive clusters with brief pauses between them. The rhythm of a fiber whip performance: crack-crack-crack, flow, crack-crack, flow, crack.

The pause windows are where the player breathes. The burst windows are where they execute or fail. Miss a single beat in a burst and the entire burst fails.

Unique mechanic: "trail matching." During each burst, Whippa leaves a light trail in a specific shape. The player's inputs must trace that shape. It's not just rhythm — it's spatial rhythm. The player is drawing with speed.

Five rounds. Each round faster. The fifth round is at the edge of human reaction time. Players will fail it multiple times. That's by design. Whippa doesn't slow down. The player speeds up.

Between attempts, Whippa doesn't flee or reset. It performs. Beautiful, full-speed fiber work in the rain, light trails cutting through water droplets. The failed player watches art while they collect themselves for the next try. No punishment. Just the implicit challenge: that's the speed you need to match.

## Party Role

Whippa is the party's interceptor. Where Cyrus is the immovable object, Whippa is the unstoppable force.

- With Whippa in the party, fleeing creatures can be cut off — Whippa races ahead and cracks across their escape path, turning them back toward the player
- Encounter prep time is reduced. Whippa's speed compresses the pre-capture approach phase.
- Other party creatures react to Whippa with nervous energy. Stavvy's bob rate increases. Hoopla (#3) tries to roll faster and wobbles out.

Special ability: **First Strike**. In any encounter, Whippa gets a free "crack" that startles the target creature, making the first beat of the capture sequence easier (wider timing window). A head start. Whippa was always going to move first.

Whippa and Fyr interaction: Whippa's speed fans Fyr's flames. When both are active, Fyr burns brighter and Whippa's light trails gain warm-toned ember particles. The fast and the hot feeding each other.

Whippa and Glowr interaction: opposite approaches to light in darkness. Glowr paints slowly, Whippa slashes fast. Both in the party at night creates alternating patterns — Glowr's slow color wash punctuated by Whippa's sharp streaks. Festival visuals.

## 3D Implementation Notes

Fiber simulation is the core challenge. Hundreds of individual strands need to:
- Follow physics (gravity, inertia, wind) during rest/slow movement
- Merge into a single coherent whip body during cracks (blend individual strand positions toward a shared spline)
- Separate realistically on deceleration (release from shared spline with individual damping values)

Implementation path: GPU-computed particle chains (compute shader). Each fiber is a chain of 20-30 linked particles. At rest, gravity dominates. During cracks, a "whip force" propagates tip-to-base, overriding individual physics with the shared crack trajectory.

Light trails: ribbon geometry per fiber, emissive material, 1.5-second fade. During cracks, the ribbons merge into a single wide trail (the visual "streak"). Additive blending. No bloom dependency — Whippa's light is sharp, not diffuse.

The handle: simple cylindrical mesh with an emissive ring at the base. The ring's color drives fiber color via a scrolling UV offset on the fiber material — color propagates from base to tip at the speed of light (visually) with a slight delay per fiber length.

Performance: fiber count can LOD — 200+ fibers up close, 50 at medium distance, single ribbon trail at far distance. The visual reads the same at all LODs because the fibers merge during fast movement anyway.

Sound sync: crack audio must be sample-accurate with the visual extension peak. Latency here breaks the illusion. Pre-trigger the audio by 1-2 frames if needed.

## Voice / Sound

Sharp. Fast. Percussive. Whippa communicates through:
- The crack: a clean, supersonic snap. The defining sound. Each crack is short — 100ms — but cuts through any ambient audio. Real fiber whips don't break the sound barrier, but Whippa does. Artistic license earned by legend status.
- Fiber flutter during slow movement: a soft, continuous rustle. Hundreds of thin strands shifting against each other. Like shuffling a deck of cards very slowly.
- The handle hum: a focused electronic buzz from the LED array. Higher pitched than Glowr's hum. More insistent.
- Impact sounds when fibers strike surfaces during happy-state play: light taps, almost musical. Different surfaces produce different pitches. Whippa discovers this and starts tapping surfaces in patterns. Accidentally making music.
- During sleep: the quietest possible rustle of fibers settling. A sound you'd miss if you weren't listening for it.

## Design Intent

Whippa is the adrenaline legendary. Where the other Tier 4 creatures test patience, observation, empathy, or comprehension, Whippa tests nerve and reflexes. It's the creature for players who play fast.

The encounter design — standing still while something dangerous spirals closer — is a direct inversion of the player's instinct. Every game teaches you to move when threatened. Whippa teaches you to hold. Fire spinners know this: the flame passes close to your body and you don't flinch because flinching moves your body into the path. Stillness is safety. The encounter encodes that lesson.

The capture sequence being speed-based with a spatial component (trail matching) prevents it from being pure twitch. Fast hands aren't enough. Fast hands that draw the right shape are. This mirrors real fiber whip manipulation: anyone can flail fast, but flowing a whip into clean, intentional patterns while maintaining speed is the actual skill.

Whippa in the party is a power fantasy. The creature that was too fast to see is now on your side. Other creatures can't run. Encounters start with an advantage. The player who proved they could hold still and then move fast gets rewarded with a creature that makes everything a little faster. Momentum as party identity.
