# #010 — Baton

> A smaller stick with a bigger chip on its shoulder.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Name | Baton |
| Prop | Baton / Mini Staff |
| Tier | 2 — Uncommon |
| Rarity | Found exploring — near Stavvy habitats but slightly further out, as if it wandered off trying to prove something |
| Animal Analog | Chihuahua — small, fast, aggressive confidence that outpaces its physical stature by a wide margin |

## Appearance

A single staff, about 2 feet long — roughly half Stavvy's length per stave. Thinner, too. Lightweight. The kind of prop that looks like it could snap if you gripped it wrong, but moves so fast that fragility becomes irrelevant.

The surface is glossy where Stavvy is matte — Baton tries harder to look polished. Tip glow is brighter per unit of length, overcompensating. The glow color runs warmer than Stavvy's (orange-tinged vs. Stavvy's cool blue-white), like a smaller engine running hotter.

Baton moves in short, sharp arcs where Stavvy moves in long, lazy ones. Same vocabulary, double the tempo, half the reach. Everything Stavvy does, Baton does faster. Not better. Faster.

Optional: one googly eye, oversized relative to the staff's length. It's always looking at Stavvy. Or at where Stavvy would be if Stavvy were here. Or at the player, checking if the player noticed the trick it just did.

## Movement & Behavior

### Idle
- Bobs up and down like Stavvy, but at twice the frequency and half the amplitude — hummingbird to Stavvy's pigeon
- Spins in place constantly — not Stavvy's lazy rotation, but a rapid twirl on its center point (baton twirling is the real-world basis)
- Periodically attempts a vertical toss — throws itself straight up, spins end over end 3-4 times, catches itself. Nails it. Immediately does it again. And again. Compulsive self-tossing
- Drifts at the player's hip height, not shoulder — shorter creature, lower orbit
- When idle near environmental objects (rocks, posts, benches), attempts to balance on them tip-down. Succeeds for 2-3 seconds. Falls. Hops back up. Tries again on a different object

### Alert (player approaches)
- Launches into a rapid trick chain: toss, spin, toss-spin combo, behind-the-back pass (passes behind an invisible back), under-the-leg (passes under an invisible leg)
- Every trick is executed at maximum speed with maximum commitment
- If the player watches without approaching further: the tricks escalate. Higher tosses. More spins per toss. A double that becomes a triple that becomes a quad
- If the player looks away: Baton moves into the player's line of sight. Resumes tricks. The performance requires an audience
- The tip glow pulses with each trick — a flashbulb demanding attention

### Happy (after capture / in party)
- Stays close to the player, closer than any other creature — almost underfoot, orbiting at shin-to-knee height
- Attempts to mirror whatever the party's largest creature is doing, scaled down to its own size. If Stavvy bobs, Baton bobs faster. If Ropey strikes, Baton lunges forward (half the distance, twice the speed, no actual dart to speak of)
- When the player performs a capture sequence on another creature, Baton performs alongside them — an unsolicited backup dancer
- Occasionally bonks into the player's leg while doing an overly ambitious trick. Bounces off. Resumes immediately. No acknowledgment of the collision
- When praised (any positive interaction), vibrates in place at high frequency — too excited to pick a single motion

### Startled
- Does not cross defensively like Stavvy. Instead: freezes mid-trick. A baton stopped mid-spin, hanging in the air at whatever angle it was at when the startle hit
- Holds the freeze for a full second — the comedic beat of "oh no"
- Then bolts. Not away from the source — toward it. Charges the threat, spinning end over end like a thrown projectile
- Reaches the threat (or where the threat was). Stops. Looks around. Nothing there (the sound has passed). Slowly drifts back to the player, acting like that was the plan all along

### Sleep
- Does not lay horizontal like Stavvy. Balances vertically on one tip — asleep but still showing off
- Wobbles slightly. A top winding down but never quite falling
- The glow dims to an ember
- If it finally falls over (low probability per cycle, but eventual): clatters to the ground, rolls once, pops back up to tip-balance without waking. Sleep-recovering. A reflex so deep it doesn't require consciousness

## How You Encounter It

Baton lives at the edges of areas where the player has already found or bonded with Stavvy. Not in the starter zone itself — just past it. As if Baton was too impatient to wait there and wandered out looking for action.

The encounter is noise-first. You hear rapid-fire tapping and whooshing — a small object moving very fast, hitting things. Follow the sound to find Baton mid-routine, performing for nobody. A rock. A tree. A patch of ground. The audience doesn't matter. The performance is compulsory.

When the player arrives, Baton doesn't startle or flee. It performs harder. This is the first creature in the game that actively wants to be caught. The challenge isn't getting close — Baton comes to you. The challenge is matching its energy.

Baton appears more often if Stavvy is in the active party. The creature has a detection radius for Stavvy specifically — it gravitates toward its larger counterpart. Finding Baton without Stavvy in your party is possible but less common. The sibling dynamic is mechanical, not just narrative.

## Capture Sequence Difficulty

**Easy.** The difficulty is keeping up.

The capture sequence is the fastest in Tier 2 — short duration, high tempo. The player needs to match Baton's energy with quick, snappy inputs. Not complex patterns — simple ones, fast. The sequence mirrors real baton manipulation: rapid wrist-driven movements, not full-arm sweeps.

Baton is forgiving on timing but demanding on speed. Slow inputs don't fail the sequence — they bore Baton. The creature visibly loses interest (stops watching the player, resumes self-entertaining). The capture timer pauses when Baton stops paying attention. The player has to speed up to re-engage.

Unique mechanic: style bonus. Any input that coincidentally mirrors one of Baton's own tricks (a spin, a toss pattern) causes Baton to freeze in shock — someone else can do that?? — and the capture progresses by 3 beats instead of 1. Players who study Baton's idle routine before attempting capture can speedrun it.

## Party Role

Baton is the party's hype creature. It doesn't have a strategic ability — it has enthusiasm. When any other creature in the party performs well (successful trick, interaction, or combat move), Baton reacts with amplified mirroring, making the moment feel bigger.

Interaction with other party members:
- Follows Stavvy like a shadow. Mimics every bob, every arc, every rotation — at 2x speed, half scale. Stavvy doesn't acknowledge this. Whether Stavvy doesn't notice or is politely ignoring it is left ambiguous
- Challenges Klubba to trick competitions. Klubba doesn't know they're competing. Baton keeps score anyway (visible tally marks drawn in the air with tip-glow trails)
- Tries to ride Diablo's string. Gets flung off. Tries again immediately. Diablo is too busy worrying about its own spin to deal with this
- Orbits Hoopla's rolling path, attempting to run alongside. Hoopla is larger and slower. Baton keeps lapping it and looking back impatiently
- Intimidated by nothing. Approaches Ropey's coiled form without hesitation. Ropey strikes. Baton dodges (by virtue of being small and fast). Approaches again. A creature too dumb to be afraid or too brave to care — the distinction is academic

Special ability: **Second Wind.** When Baton is in the active party and the player fails a capture sequence, the cooldown before retry is halved. Baton's philosophy: you fell? Get up. Try again. Faster.

## 3D Implementation Notes

Single cylinder geometry, same shader family as Staff3D but scaled to 40% length and 60% radius. Tip emissive intensity = Stavvy's baseline * 1.8, color temperature shifted +800K.

Spin: constant local-Y rotation at 720 deg/s (2 full rotations per second) during idle. Self-toss: upward impulse + end-over-end tumble (rotation on local-X during ballistic phase). Catch: position snaps to pre-toss hover point when Y velocity crosses zero on descent.

Tip-balance: physics constraint pins one tip to a surface contact point. Angular deviation from vertical feeds a PD controller that applies corrective torque. Sleep wobble: reduce controller gain by 80%, let natural perturbation accumulate until tip deviation exceeds 15 degrees (fall threshold).

Mirroring behavior: sample target creature's root node transform delta per frame. Apply same delta to Baton's root node, scaled by 0.5 (position) and 2.0 (rotation speed). Lag: 3-frame delay on the sample, creating a "following" feel rather than synchronization.

Proximity to Stavvy: if Stavvy is in the party, Baton's idle orbit center biases toward Stavvy's position (weighted average: 60% player, 40% Stavvy). Creates natural clustering without hard-coded following.

## Voice / Sound

No speech. Baton communicates through:
- Rapid high-pitched whooshes (small object, fast air displacement — the sound of effort)
- A hollow wooden tap when bonking into things (lighter than Stavvy's thunk — more of a "tok")
- A sharp click on successful self-toss catch (the satisfying snap of a baton landing in palm, but there's no palm — the click is the sound of commitment meeting gravity)
- A buzzing vibration when excited (the staff spinning so fast it becomes a hum — smaller diameter = higher pitch than Stavvy)
- Rapid-fire tok-tok-tok when performing trick chains (each trick punctuated, a drummer doing fills)

Baton is the loudest creature relative to its size. The audio footprint is disproportionate. You hear Baton coming the way you hear a chihuahua barking from three rooms away.

## Design Intent

Baton exists to be the creature the player underestimates and then loves. The setup: it's smaller than Stavvy, tries too hard, can't stop moving, and has the emotional regulation of a caffeinated terrier. The payoff: it's the creature that never lets the player give up.

The sibling dynamic with Stavvy is the core emotional hook. Stavvy is the dog that sits beside you. Baton is the younger sibling that saw the dog sitting beside you and decided to stand on its head beside you instead, because sitting is for creatures that don't have tricks to show. Stavvy's loyalty is quiet. Baton's loyalty is deafening.

Gameplay-wise, Baton teaches speed as a capture variable. Tier 1 creatures and the earlier Tier 2 entries reward patience, observation, or rhythm. Baton rewards raw tempo. The player who learns to switch between Ribbini's slow flow and Baton's rapid-fire snaps has the full toolkit for Tier 3.

The design intent behind the startled behavior — charging the threat instead of fleeing — is the creature's entire personality distilled into one reaction. Baton doesn't know it's small. Baton doesn't know it should be afraid. Baton knows one thing: go forward, go fast, don't stop. The player either finds this annoying or endearing, and the ones who find it endearing will never take Baton out of their party.
