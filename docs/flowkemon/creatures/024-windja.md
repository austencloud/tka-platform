# #024 — Windja

> The wind picks up. Something takes shape in the air. Then the wind dies, and it's gone.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Name | Windja |
| Prop | Windsinger / Flow Cloth |
| Tier | 4 — Legendary |
| Rarity | Exists only in wind. No wind, no Windja. The rarest encounter in the game. |
| Animal Analog | Ghost — not alive in the conventional sense, defined by absence, present only through environment |

## Appearance

Fabric. Flowing, translucent fabric with no fixed form, no frame, no handle, no structure. The shape is entirely determined by air currents. In strong wind, Windja is a billowing column — tall, dramatic, edges snapping. In gentle breeze, it's a low ripple across the ground, barely distinguishable from grass. In gusts, it whips into violent angular shapes and then softens as the gust passes.

The fabric is iridescent. Not a fixed color — the surface shifts between pearl, silver, pale blue, and near-transparency depending on the angle of light and the wind speed. At certain moments, when the fabric goes taut and catches light at the right angle, it's opaque and solid-looking. A moment later, a shift in wind, and it's nearly invisible.

The edges of the fabric are indeterminate. Where does Windja end and the air begin? The outermost edges fray into wisps that dissolve into environment particles. The creature has no clear boundary.

No googly eyes. No face. No front. Windja is oriented entirely by wind direction. The player's brain will try to find a face in the fabric folds. Sometimes, briefly, the creases align in a way that suggests features. Then the wind shifts and the suggestion vanishes.

## Movement & Behavior

### Idle
- Windja doesn't idle. It flows. Its behavior is the wind's behavior.
- In steady wind: long, graceful undulations. The fabric stretches out to its full extent (3-4m), riding the current. Moves like a manta ray in water — slow, wide, effortless.
- In gusty wind: rapid shape changes. Snapping between forms. A flag one moment, a spiral the next, a compressed ball caught in a vortex, then unfurling again.
- In light breeze: low to the ground. Barely moving. The player might mistake it for a discarded cloth.
- In no wind: Windja doesn't exist. The fabric collapses to the ground and becomes a terrain texture. Literally unrenderable as a creature. It's a scarf on the ground.

### Alert (player approaches)
- The wind intensifies in Windja's immediate area — localized gust, as if the creature is generating its own weather
- The fabric lifts and orientates toward the player. A wall of silk facing you, rippling.
- The edges extend outward, framing the player's view. Like curtains parting, except the curtains are alive and the stage is you.
- If the player reaches out (approaches within 2m): the fabric brushes past them. Through them. Windja has no collision. It flows around the player like water around a stone. The player feels nothing. The fabric feels everything.

### Happy (after capture / in party)
- Wraps loosely around the player, trailing behind like a cloak or scarf. Not restrictive — decorative. The player acquires a flowing train of iridescent fabric.
- Responds to player movement speed: walking, it drapes. Running, it streams behind. Jumping, it parachutes momentarily.
- In open windy areas, detaches from the player and performs — full windsinger choreography, riding thermals, diving and climbing. Returns to the player when the wind settles.
- Other party creatures interact with Windja as if it's weather. Stavvy's tips flutter. Ribbini (#9) syncs wavelength with Windja's undulations. Fyr's flames lean toward Windja's draft.

### Startled
- The fabric goes rigid. Freezes mid-billow in a shape that defies the current wind — holding a form that should be impossible given the air movement. Tense silk.
- Holds for one second.
- Then: violent whip. The fabric snaps in the direction of the threat, cracking like a flag in a gale. Not an attack — a flinch. The whole creature becomes a single sharp gesture and then immediately softens back to flow.

### Sleep
- Windja sleeps only when there is no wind. It has no choice. When the air stills completely, the fabric descends and settles on the ground.
- Draped over whatever terrain is beneath it — a rock, a bush, flat ground — conforming perfectly to the surface topology. Like a sheet thrown over furniture in an abandoned house.
- The iridescence dims to a faint shimmer. If the player didn't know Windja was there, they would not find it.
- The first gust of returning wind lifts one edge. Then another. The waking is gradual. Windja reassembles itself from cloth to creature as the air returns.

## How You Encounter It

Windja only spawns on the highest points of the map during sustained strong wind events. Hilltops, cliff edges, mountain ridges — places where the wind is unobstructed and the sky is wide open.

Prerequisite: the player must have caught Ribbini (#9, the ribbon creature, Tier 2). Ribbini is fabric on a stick. Windja is fabric set free. The evolution is conceptual.

Additional condition: the in-game weather must produce wind above a threshold for at least 5 continuous minutes. Light wind won't summon Windja. It needs real force. Players will learn to check the weather and head for high ground when storms approach.

The encounter is the hardest to trigger in the game because two variables must align: the player's position (high altitude, open terrain) and the world's weather state (strong sustained wind). Neither is player-controlled. The player can only be in the right place and wait for the right conditions. Patience measured in real-time sessions, not in-game objectives.

What the player sees first: movement in the sky that isn't clouds. A shape forming in the wind above the ridgeline. It could be a plastic bag. It could be a loose tarp. It's neither. It's too deliberate. The shapes are too beautiful. No litter moves like that.

Approaching the ridgeline, the wind hits the player's character model (clothes flutter, hair moves, movement speed reduces against headwind). Windja is above, riding the updraft, performing the flow cloth art of shaping fabric around invisible air. The player climbs toward it.

Windja descends to meet the player only if the player reaches the highest accessible point and stands in the full wind. You can't observe Windja from shelter. You have to stand where it lives.

## Capture Sequence Difficulty

Windja's capture sequence is tied to the wind itself. The beats don't come at fixed intervals — they come when the wind gusts.

Each gust triggers a beat window. The player watches Windja's shape change and responds during the moment of maximum billow. Between gusts, no beats — just the player and the wind and the waiting.

This makes the capture sequence unpredictable in rhythm. Some gusts come in rapid succession. Some have long pauses. The player has to sync to weather, not to a metronome. Musical training helps less than wind-reading helps.

Unique mechanic: the wind can die mid-sequence. If the sustained wind drops below threshold during the capture attempt, Windja begins to descend. The fabric goes limp. The creature is falling asleep. The player has a 10-second window to complete whatever beats remain, and each beat in dying wind requires harder input (tighter timing, more precise). Windja is fighting to stay awake long enough for you to finish.

If the wind dies completely before capture completes: Windja sleeps. The fabric settles. The player is standing on a hilltop next to a scarf. They can pick it up (not capture — just hold). The scarf is warm. Windja is in there, dormant. The player carries the dormant fabric until the wind returns. When it does — whenever it does, wherever the player is — the fabric lifts from their hands and Windja is back. The sequence resumes where it left off.

This means a single capture attempt can span multiple play sessions. The player might carry sleeping Windja for hours of real time before conditions align again. No other creature demands this kind of commitment.

## Party Role

Windja changes the game's relationship with weather. With Windja in the party:

- Headwind movement penalties are removed. The player moves at full speed in any wind direction. Windja parts the air.
- Wind-dependent environmental puzzles (windmills, wind-powered platforms, sail mechanisms) activate automatically when the player approaches, regardless of current wind state. Windja provides its own wind.
- During wind events, Windja extends the player's perception range — the fabric acts as a sensory organ, detecting creatures and items at distances beyond normal visibility.

Special ability: **Zephyr**. The player can release Windja to scout ahead. The fabric flies along wind currents, revealing map areas around the player's position. The range depends on real wind strength. In calm conditions, Windja barely lifts off. In gale conditions, it can scout the entire visible horizon. The tradeoff: while scouting, Windja isn't with the player. You sent it away. It comes back when the wind does.

Windja and Fyr interaction: Wind feeds flame. Fyr's fire flares dramatically when Windja is nearby. The increased flame produces more light, more heat, more visual drama — but also more danger. Fyr's avoidance radius increases. The combination is powerful and volatile.

Windja and Puppetta interaction: Windja's air currents disturb Puppetta's strings. When both are in the party, Puppetta's movement becomes more erratic — the invisible strings sway in Windja's draft. Puppetta tries to compensate. The result is Puppetta's most natural-looking movement: fighting wind, stumbling, recovering. Almost human.

## 3D Implementation Notes

Cloth simulation is the entire creature. No skeleton, no rigid body, no mesh deformation from bones. Pure cloth physics.

Implementation: GPU cloth simulation with wind force inputs sampled from the game's weather system. The cloth is a high-resolution quad grid (64x64 minimum for the edge-fray quality) with:
- Variable mass per vertex (edges lighter than center, for the dissolving-boundary effect)
- Wind force applied as a directional field with turbulence noise
- Self-collision to prevent fold-through (expensive, can be reduced to sphere-proxy at distance)
- No fixed anchor points during flight. The cloth is free. Pinning is only used during sleep (terrain drape) and happy-wrap (player attachment points)

The iridescence: a thin-film interference shader driven by view angle and surface normal. The normal is chaotic (cloth simulation) so the color shifts are organic and unpredictable. Base colors: pearl, ice blue, silver. At extreme angles: faint gold.

Transparency: alpha driven by fabric stretch. Taut fabric = more opaque. Slack fabric = more transparent. This naturally produces the "visible in strong wind, invisible in calm" behavior without a special state toggle.

The "no collision" during alert (fabric flows through the player): disable player collision for Windja's cloth specifically, but keep self-collision active. The fabric wraps around the player's collision volume via a proximity force field rather than physical intersection.

Performance critical: cloth sim at 64x64 is ~4096 vertices at physics rate. This is the creature's budget. LOD: reduce grid resolution at distance (32x32, then 16x16). At far distance, replace with a billboard particle sheet that approximates the fabric silhouette.

The sleeping-to-waking transition (cloth conforms to terrain, then lifts edge by edge) needs a blended state between terrain-draped static mesh and active cloth sim. Crossfade over 3 seconds as wind ramps.

## Voice / Sound

Wind is Windja's voice. Not prop sounds — environment sounds.
- The rushing of air through fabric: the primary communication channel. Pitch and volume tied to wind speed. In gale: a roar, fabric snapping. In breeze: a whisper, silk sliding.
- A low, sustained note when Windja goes taut in strong wind — the sound a flag makes when it's pulled fully flat. A single clean tone from vibrating fabric.
- Flutter: rapid staccato when transitioning between shapes. The fabric is talking faster than you can parse.
- Silence when sleeping. Absolute. The wind has stopped. Windja's voice is gone because the medium is gone.
- The waking sound: the quietest possible rustle. One edge lifting. The first word of a sentence that continues as long as the wind holds.

No electronic sounds. No manufactured audio. Everything Windja produces is acoustic — fabric, air, tension. The most organic sound design in the entire creature roster.

## Design Intent

Windja is the creature that might not exist.

Every other legendary has physical presence. Cyrus is enormous. Fyr is hot. Glowr produces light. Whippa makes sound. Puppetta has wooden mass. Oxbow has topology. Windja has fabric and whatever the wind gives it. Take away the wind and Windja is a cloth on the ground. Is that still a creature? Does it have agency when its form depends entirely on an external force?

This existential ambiguity is the design point. Windja is the contemplative legendary. The player who catches it has engaged with a question the game never answers: where does the wind end and the creature begin?

The multi-session capture possibility is the boldest design choice. Most games would never let a capture attempt span days of real time. Windja demands it because Windja is the creature that teaches the player the wind's schedule. You can't rush weather. You carry a dormant piece of silk in your inventory and you wait, and when the wind returns and the fabric lifts from your hands, you feel something no instant capture could produce.

Windja as the final creature in the roster (#024) is intentional. It's the creature that dissolves the boundary between game system and environment. Every creature before it was a thing in the world. Windja is the world itself, temporarily taking shape. The player's journey from Stavvy (a pair of sticks that follow you) to Windja (a piece of sky that might be alive) is the emotional arc of Flowkemon: you started by caring about objects, and you ended by caring about air.
