# #019 — Fyr

> The sun sets. The world catches fire. Something is burning in the field, and it's alive.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Name | Fyr |
| Prop | Fire Props (poi, staff, fans — all kevlar-wicked, all lit) |
| Tier | 4 — Legendary |
| Rarity | Appears at dusk. Burns until dawn. One chance per night. |
| Animal Analog | Phoenix — rebirth cycle, dangerous beauty, demands respect |

## Appearance

A cluster of burning props moving as a single organism. Two fire poi orbit a central fire staff spine, with fire fans flanking — the whole assembly suggests a bird made of flame. The kevlar wicks are visible at the core of each flame, blackened and soaked. Real fire behavior: the flames gutter in wind, flare when Fyr spins faster, and leave actual scorch marks on the ground.

The creature is a light source. It casts dynamic shadows, illuminates nearby terrain and creatures in flickering orange, and produces visible heat distortion above it.

No googly eyes. The fire is the face. Flame height and color shift express emotion — tall yellow-white is alert, low blue is calm, erratic orange is agitated.

## Movement & Behavior

### Idle
- Slow, meditative spin. The poi orbit in extended patterns — the fire spinner's "warm-up flow"
- Drifts along paths, burning. Grass chars behind it. Small environmental fires ignite and die in its wake.
- Other creatures maintain a 15m radius. They know.
- The light cast creates a moving circle of visibility in the dusk world — Fyr is a walking lighthouse

### Alert (player approaches)
- Flames flare — height doubles, color shifts toward white
- Spin rate increases. The poi tighten their orbits.
- Heat distortion intensifies. The air warps visibly between Fyr and the player.
- If the player comes closer than 5m without initiating capture: Fyr erupts. A burst of fire in all directions. Not damage — a warning. The player's screen whites out momentarily, then Fyr has relocated 50m away.

### Happy (after capture / in party)
- Flames calm to a steady, warm orange. Campfire energy.
- Orbits near the player at night, illuminating the path ahead
- Performs slow fire trails — the poi trace figure-8s that leave fading ember paths in the air
- Other party creatures gradually reduce their avoidance radius over time. They learn Fyr won't burn them. It takes days.

### Startled
- All flames extinguish simultaneously. Sudden darkness.
- A beat of nothing. The player's adjusted night vision is gone.
- Then: reignition. Every wick flares at once, brighter than before. The re-light is the flinch response.

### Sleep
- Fyr doesn't sleep in the conventional sense. It smolders.
- Flames reduce to ember glow along the wicks — visible only as faint red-orange dots
- The heat shimmer remains. The air above Fyr still warps.
- Occasional flicker — like breathing on coals

## How You Encounter It

Fyr appears only during the dusk transition — the 10-minute window when the game world shifts from day to night. The player must be in an open-field biome. No caves, no forests. Fire needs air.

Prerequisite: the player must have caught at least one creature from each lower tier (Common, Uncommon, Rare). Fyr doesn't appear to beginners. Fire spinning is an advanced discipline, and Fyr respects that progression.

The encounter begins with smell — a flavor text prompt: "Something is burning." Then light. A glow on the horizon, wrong for sunset, moving against the sky's gradient. As the player approaches, the ambient soundtrack fades and is replaced by the crackle and whoosh of spinning flame.

Every creature in the area is already fleeing. The player walks toward the thing everything else is running from.

Fyr is performing. Not for the player — for itself. The fire flow is beautiful, deliberate, practiced. Approaching interrupts nothing. Fyr finishes its sequence, then turns the flame toward you.

One encounter per night cycle. If the player fails or flees, Fyr burns until dawn, then extinguishes. Gone. Try again tomorrow at dusk.

## Capture Sequence Difficulty

Fyr's capture sequence must be performed in the fire's light.

The sequence is fast. Fire spinning is about momentum — stop moving and the flame dies. The beats come quicker than any other capture sequence, and the timing windows are tight. Hesitation fails.

Unique mechanic: the capture sequence has a "fuel timer." Like a real fire prop, Fyr's flame is burning down. The player can see the wicks shortening. Take too long, and Fyr burns out — not a failure exactly, but Fyr extinguishes and vanishes. You weren't fast enough to match its energy.

The sequence itself uses high-momentum vocabulary: continuous spins, no static holds, beat transitions that require maintaining flow rather than hitting discrete positions. It mirrors fire spinning's real constraint — you can't pause mid-performance.

Failing doesn't trigger aggression. Fyr simply resumes its solo flow, turning away. You weren't ready. The fire doesn't care.

## Party Role

Fyr is a walking environmental modifier. With Fyr in the party:

- Night exploration becomes viable — Fyr illuminates a wide radius
- Creatures that only appear in darkness may flee from Fyr's light (tradeoff)
- Cold-biome penalties are nullified
- Other party creatures slowly acclimate to Fyr's heat radius, gaining a "fire-forged" status over time that makes them braver in encounters

Special ability: **Reignition**. Once per encounter, if the player fails a capture sequence, Fyr can "re-light" the attempt — a second chance that costs Fyr visible flame height. Fyr diminishes to give you another shot. It remembers what it cost to learn fire spinning.

Fyr and Glowr have a unique interaction: when both are in the party at night, Fyr's warm light and Glowr's cool bioluminescence create a dual-tone lighting system. The world looks different with both active. Other creatures react to the combined light with awe rather than fear.

## 3D Implementation Notes

Particle system-heavy creature. Each prop component (poi x2, staff x1, fans x2) is a mesh with attached fire particle emitters. Fire particles need:
- Directional emission tied to spin velocity (flames trail behind motion)
- Color temperature ramp: blue core, orange body, yellow tips, white flare on speed-up
- Wind response: particle direction offsets based on world wind vector
- Ground scorch: decal system for temporary burn marks on terrain

Dynamic point light parented to Fyr's root, with intensity and color driven by flame state. Shadow-casting enabled — this is expensive but the shadows sell the creature.

Heat distortion: screen-space refraction shader in a radius around Fyr. Intensity scales with flame state. Disabled at distance to save performance.

The startled sequence (extinguish-then-reignite) needs a custom particle burst for the reignition — not a fade-in, an explosion outward from the wicks.

## Voice / Sound

Fire is loud. Fyr communicates through:
- Sustained whoosh of spinning flame (the signature fire-spinning sound — air feeding fire in rhythmic pulses)
- Sharp crack when flame flares (the sound of a fire fan snapping open while lit)
- Low crackle during idle (campfire)
- Silence during the startled extinguish — a held breath
- Explosive roar on reignition
- A steady, quiet hiss while smoldering (sleep state) — like a pilot light that never quite goes out

The sound design should make the player feel warmth. Not metaphorical. The audio should trick the brain into sensing heat.

## Design Intent

Fyr is the creature that represents danger and beauty in the same frame. Fire spinning is the most visually dramatic flow art, and also the one that can actually hurt you. Fyr carries that duality.

The dusk-only appearance creates a ritual. Players will watch the in-game clock, position themselves in open fields, and wait. The wait is part of the experience. Fire performers know this — you don't rush the lighting. You prepare, you check the wind, you wait for the right moment.

Fyr's capture sequence being a momentum test rather than a precision test distinguishes it from Cyrus's endurance trial. Different legendary, different demand. Cyrus asks if you're patient enough. Fyr asks if you're brave enough.

When Fyr joins the party, the game literally looks different. The world is brighter. The other creatures are visible. The player traded darkness for firelight. That should feel like a real choice with real consequences — some things only appear in the dark, and Fyr's light might scare them away.
