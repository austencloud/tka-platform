## The six wings

### 1 · SPLIT-SAME (Water) — cases A, B, C

- **Teaching sentence:** Two hands share one path, half a cycle apart — each
  hand is the other's echo.
- **Unique observable:** the echo made visible: the still pool doubles every
  figure, and a reflection is exactly what split-same is in time — the same
  shape, displaced.
- **Barrier:** still water. **Opener:** dual-wheel staging (`6bfjxuoT`).
- **Signature move (approved lineage):** the dive-through passage arriving
  at the mirror-pool parapet.
- **Payoff:** A, B and C doubled in the pool from the parapet.

### 2 · SPLIT-OPPOSITE (Fire) — cases from the split-opp catalog rows

- **Teaching sentence:** Two hands a half-cycle apart, arcing opposite ways —
  never together in time, never together in direction: pure alternation.
- **Unique observable:** alternation as light — when one is up the other is
  down, like flames trading.
- **Barrier:** heat and shrine gates. 
- **Signature move (PROPOSAL, inherits `jl8TveF5` + cinder-court shrines):**
  the darkening procession — each shrine's flames self-extinguish as the next
  ignites (the lighting itself performs split-opposite), ending in total
  extinction with green growth circling the cold trench into Earth (canon).
- **Payoff:** the last lit shrine seen across the dark of the extinguished
  ones.

### 3 · TOGETHER-SAME (Earth) — cases G, H, I

- **Teaching sentence:** Two hands in sync, arcing the same way — unison:
  two hands, one motion.
- **Unique observable:** from above, unison reads as a single shape — the
  overlook is the pedagogy.
- **Barrier:** elevation. 
- **Signature move (approved lineage):** the terrace climb above the cases.
- **Payoff:** the final ensemble sightline — three unison figures aligned
  (kept from the Earth contract).

### 4 · TOGETHER-OPPOSITE (Air) — cases from the tog-opp catalog rows

- **Teaching sentence:** Two hands in sync, arcing opposite ways — mirror
  symmetry in space, moving as one moment.
- **Unique observable:** the mirror axis (canon: opposite-direction wings
  carry one) — the room and the motion share it.
- **Barrier:** a void crossed on bridges.
- **Signature move (PROPOSAL):** the twin crossing — the path splits into two
  mirrored bridges around the central void and rejoins; the visitor's own
  route performs together-opposite. Performers on ledges across the void,
  framed against depth.
- **Payoff:** from the rejoin point, both bridges and all cases in one
  mirrored view.

### 5 · QUARTER-SAME (Sun) — cases S, T, U, V

- **Teaching sentence:** Two hands a quarter-cycle apart, arcing the same
  way — a chase that never closes.
- **Unique observable:** four-fold rotational symmetry — four letters, four
  quarters (inherits the 08-05 mandala idea).
- **Barrier:** the central light shaft/atrium.
- **Signature move (PROPOSAL):** the quarter circuit — the path rings the
  shaft in four quarter-turn landings, one case per quarter, the mandala
  floor below.
- **Payoff:** from the fourth landing, all four cases around the lit mandala.

### 6 · QUARTER-OPPOSITE (Moon) — cases MP, NQ, OR

- **Teaching sentence:** A quarter-cycle apart, arcing opposite ways — the
  waxing and waning relationship.
- **Unique observable:** phases: quarter offsets are what moon phases are.
- **Barrier:** sealed observation glass — no air out there (the regolith
  plain becomes the diorama).
- **Signature move (PROPOSAL):** the observatory gallery — a long sealed
  window traverse where the plain reveals in phase-lit segments, waxing to
  the full view.
- **Payoff:** the full window — all three performers on the plain at once.

## Museum-scale notes

- **The cave's internal barrier arc previews the macro arc for free:** the
  walk order runs natural media (water, heat, elevation, void) → architectural
  (light shaft) → literal glass (Moon). The cave ends on the hardest, most
  institutional barrier, setting the trajectory the later historical wings
  continue. No redesign needed — the walk order already does it.
- **The first crossable barrier belongs to the Crumble (PROPOSAL):** the
  Order never softens its casework — decay does. The player's first "I could
  step over this" moment is a collapsed rail or cracked vitrine in the
  Crumble era, institutional death read as unintentional softening, before
  the Scribe rooms remove barriers on purpose.
- **Seam handoffs (PROPOSAL — art-pass level, recorded as intent):**
  elements hand off through natural phenomena. Entry→Water: stream sounds
  from the wall. Water→Fire: the last surface steams; heat shimmer. 
  Fire→Earth: extinction → green growth (canon, verbatim). Earth→Air: roots
  thin, the floor falls away, wind. Air→Sun: the void brightens; the first
  shaft of light. Sun→Moon: noon dims to silver night. Moon→Egypt: dawn —
  first warm desert light.
