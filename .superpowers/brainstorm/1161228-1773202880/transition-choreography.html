Disassemble / Reassemble Transition

How should the canvas animate between assembled and disassembled states?

A

Clone & Split

The single canvas visually "clones" into three copies. The blue and red hands separate from the original — blue slides down-left, red slides down-right — while simultaneously scaling to 50%. The hero slides up slightly. All three canvases keep animating throughout.

Pros

  • Feels like pulling apart a mechanism
  • User sees the hands physically separate
  • Animation never pauses — seamless continuity

Cons

  • Needs overlay/clone elements during transition
  • Most complex to implement (fake canvases during morph)
B

Shrink & Reveal

The assembled canvas shrinks to hero size (slides up, scales to ~67% width). As it moves, two empty slots fade in below. Then the blue and red canvases fade/scale in from 0% to 100% in their slots. Two-phase: shrink hero, then reveal children.

Pros

  • Simpler — no clone trickery needed
  • Clear two-step sequence is easy to follow
  • Works with existing AnimatorCanvas instances

Cons

  • Brief moment where small slots are empty (less seamless)
  • Feels less "mechanical" — more like a UI transition
C

Crossfade Morph

The assembled layout crossfades directly to the disassembled layout. Both exist simultaneously during the transition with opposing opacity. Simple, fast, clean — like a page turn.

Pros

  • Simplest to implement
  • Fast — no waiting for multi-stage animation
  • Familiar pattern (modal/page transitions)

Cons

  • Doesn't communicate the "pulling apart" concept
  • Feels generic — not delightful or educational
  • Misses the opportunity to show what's happening

Your Vision (for reference)

"The grid, arrow, and prop elements all duplicate while the animation stays consistent. They each animate with a translation down to their appropriate areas, scaling down while doing so. The hero animates upwards and scales to its new location. The animation which might be playing continues seamlessly."

→ This maps closest to Option A (Clone & Split)
