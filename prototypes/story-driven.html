A combo that clicked. A pattern someone showed you between sets. The way two props moved together that you'd never seen before.

You told yourself you'd remember it.

It's Wednesday now.

You remember it started with a split-time antispin. Or was it a cateye? The entrance was something with your left hand going... no. Right hand.

The shape is gone. The timing is gone. You're left with a feeling and no way to get it back.

This has been happening your entire flow career.

You've tried.

Video.

You recorded it at the jam. Shaky phone footage, bad lighting, someone's elbow in frame. You watched it back six times and still can't tell what your right hand is doing at beat three.

Verbal description.

"It's like a butterfly but your right hand does a thing." You posted it in a group chat. Nobody knew what you meant. Someone replied with an unrelated video of a contact roll.

Memory.

You drilled it 50 times before bed. By morning, your muscle memory kept the first four beats and improvised the rest. Close enough, you told yourself. It wasn't.

Musicians solved this problem 1,000 years ago.

They wrote it down.
