2 · The timeline, stage by stage

Each card shows the same moment from the front (staff orientation in the plane) and from above (where the plane is, fore/aft). Thumb end in red, pinky end in blue.

① Thumb end straight down — arm at 0

The plane is bisecting the body. Nothing needs clearance: the staff is vertical, off to the side past the shoulder. From here the thumb end begins to rise — its path up sweeps through the inboard region.

② Thumb end sweeping inboard — arm at +45° forward

While the thumb end comes up through the inboard side, the arm holds a forward 45° from the body. That angle is the whole trick: the plane is downstage of the body, so the thumb end passes in front of the chest and face with clearance — the staff never has to bend out of the wall plane.

③ Thumb end straight up, pinky end straight down — arm back at 0

Before the pinky end has begun to rise, the arm has returned to zero: extended directly from the body, no forward or backward angle. At this exact moment the plane — the visible grid and the plane the prop is tethered to — is bisecting the body in half again.

④ Pinky end sweeping inboard — arm at 45° behind

The very next thing after the bisecting moment: as the pinky end comes up, the arm moves 45° behind, going upstage. The plane is now behind the body, so the pinky end naturally has nothing in its way as it sweeps past the back and head — again with no plane change required of the staff.
