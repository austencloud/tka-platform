The Earth Room — Three Floor Plans · 2026-08-04

The visitor leaves the First Fire through the amphitheatre's east door and wades east down a grass-and-wildflower gully, waist-high stems brushing past, into the only room in the wing that is viewed from above. The barrier is a vertical drop: three performers stand on a floor six metres below a continuous rim, and there is no way down. Beyond and below them the ground keeps going, terrace after terrace, into haze. Performers are GGGG, HHHH and IIII in their together-timing same-direction variations (Tog-Same, beta-to-beta): three figures hitting the same downbeat with hands converging on one point each, which is a reading that only comes free in plan view. All three plans share the same doors: enter west from Fire (fireToEarth), exit south to Air (earthToAir). North is UP on every plan. 1 grid square = 2 m. Bay ≈ 34 m east–west × 24 m north–south.

Plan A — The Gully and the Ring · continuous rim circuit · ⌀14 m void · canyon read through an east breach

Plan B — The Balcony and the Open North · closed rim · visually open north · canyon recedes BEHIND the performers

Plan C — The Stepped Gully and the Long Canyon · 20 × 11 m trough · two facing balconies · canyon runs out past the exit

A · The Gully and the Ring

A closed ring around a round hole. Continuous 3.2 m rim, ⌀14 m void, four walls. The canyon is a side view through a breach in the east wall, read from the opposite rim.

  • Element beat: wade the gully, arrive at a rim, look straight down.
  • Money shot: the fallen slab on the south rim — trio lit by the aven, dark rock foreground.
  • For: simplest geometry, one circular blocking predicate, walkable in every direction, matches the approved concept program almost exactly.
  • Against: the vista and the performers are in different directions. The visitor has to turn away from the trio to get the canyon, so the room reads as two separate ideas rather than one frame.

B · The Balcony and the Open North

Same drop, one wall visually removed. The authored shell opens north. The rim terminates in a 0.9 m boulder parapet and the ground beyond steps away in four visual shelves to −25 and into haze. A 3 m slab cantilevers into the void from the south balcony.

  • Element beat: gully → arrive on the north ledge behind the trio → come round the west arc → the canyon opens as you reach the slab.
  • Money shot: from the slab, the three figures 6 m down and the canyon receding past them for what looks like a kilometre. One frame, both ideas.
  • For: the vista sits behind the performers, which is what "endless" was asked to mean. Arriving behind them holds the reveal back one beat.
  • Against: the backdrop shelves are pure set dressing and a C-rim loses the "walk all the way round" relationship. The recommended closed-ring version keeps normal north-wall collision behind an authored parapet, so the bay stamper does not need a new shape.

C · The Stepped Gully and the Long Canyon

You look down the canyon, not across it. The void is a 20 × 11 m trough running north–south, performers strung along its axis, two facing balconies joined at the north end, and the canyon continues south past the exit.

  • Element beat: the gully itself descends in three grass shelves, so the visitor is already going down before the drop appears.
  • Money shot: the north cross-ledge, looking down the trough with all three in a line and the canyon running out beyond them.
  • For: two balconies means the trio can be read from both sides; the exit on the canyon axis is a genuinely good closing image.
  • Against: a line-of-three along the view axis defeats the whole reason to look down. Tog-Same reads because three identical figures sit side by side hitting one beat; in a receding line, the far one is 15 m away and half-occluded by the near one. It also spends the bay's whole 24 m on rim.

Recommendation — B, with A's ring closed across the parapet

Build the Balcony, and make the north ledge walkable so the circuit closes. B is right about the one thing that matters: the canyon has to be behind the performers, or the visitor has to choose between the exhibit and the view. A is right about the rim: a continuous circuit is what makes "there is no way down" felt rather than stated, because you can walk the entire edge and never find a stair. Those two are compatible. Keep B's open north flank, and run a 2.6 m ledge along the inside of the boulder parapet so the rim is unbroken all the way round. Keep the compiled north-wall collision, suppress its tile mesh, and place the authored parapet just inside it. The visual shelves remain outside the walkable terrain bay.

Why C is out: it is the most picturesque plan and the worst exhibit. The concept doc's argument for viewing Earth from above is that plan view turns Tog-Same into a free reading — three identical figures, one downbeat, hands converging to a literal dot each. That reading needs the three side by side and equidistant. C puts them in a line running away from you.

Order of beats. Gully (wade, 10 m, one bend so Fire's light is gone) → the ground opens on the left and you are on a ledge with the trio below and slightly behind you, seen from the back → west arc, the figures rotate into three-quarter view and the aven's dust column comes into the frame → the south slab, and the canyon opens past them → out the south-east notch to Air.

The two numbers graybox has to prove. Eye height is floor + 1.60. From the slab (−1.1) that is +0.50. The protected viewing line is 1 m into the void; the fractured outer 2 m of slab are blocked scenic nose. From an eye 0.35 m behind that line, the performers' feet at −7.4 are 7.9 m down and the nearest station is 4.35 m out horizontally, so the required depression angle is 61.2°. A barrier of height h at 0.35 m in front of the eye blocks anything steeper than atan((1.60 − h) / 0.35), so the slab's lip must be at most 0.96 m and is drawn at 0.45 m, which clears with about 12° of margin. On the standard south rim (−1.4, eye +0.20), the nearest station is 5.35 m from the eye and sits at 54.9°. The unrounded maximum parapet is 1.10 m, but the build cap is 1.07 m to retain collision tolerance; it is drawn at 0.90 m for 8.5° of angular margin. A 1.1 m guard rail consumes all tolerance. Second number: pulling the station line 2 m south of the void centre puts the chest slant range to the three at 7.7 / 8.7 / 8.7 m, which is Fire's fissure reading distance rather than the 14 m a centred ⌀16 m void would have given.

Open questions for the walk

  1. Does the felt downbeat survive the re-skin? A thud through a grass rim is a different animal from a thud through bare rock. Ship it as a toggle and judge it.
  2. Parapet at 0.90 m clears the south-rim sightline by 8.5°. Is that enough, or does the whole rim want the slab's 0.45 m lip and a wider standoff instead?
  3. Four backdrop shelves, or three plus more haze? Fewer, hazier shelves probably read as deeper.
  4. Grass on the rim as well as the gully, or does the rim stay bare rock so the gully keeps its contrast?
  5. Fireflies in a daylit room, or only in the gully's shaded stretch?
