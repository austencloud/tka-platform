The Fire Room — Three Floor Plans · 2026-08-04

The visitor arrives from the Drowned Gallery still dripping, through the carved threshold on the grotto's east wall. Fire's performers are the three compound pairs DJ, EK, FL (JD/KE/LF depending on start), in their split-timing variations: the two props stay 180° out of phase for the whole cycle — one hits the downbeat as the other crests — while rotating in opposite senses (DJ = pro/pro, EK = anti/anti, FL = hybrid). Which timing a pair gets depends on which specific alpha/beta variations it moves between; the together-timing variations of these same pairs belong to Air. Water taught one variable across three single letters; Fire teaches the round trip. Water's grammar was "feel the element first-person, then read it performed." Fire's version of feeling the element is heat, rhythm, and light that arrives only when the fire allows it. All three plans share the same doors: enter west from the grotto, exit east to Earth. North is UP on every plan. 1 square = 2 m.

Plan A — The Ashwalk · lava-lake crossing · ~26 × 18 m · serpentine

Plan B — The Kiln · vertical pulse chamber · ~21 m round, 14 m tall · descending spiral

Plan C — The First Fire · darkness amphitheater · ~24 × 20 m · the ancestral fire jam

A · The Ashwalk

You cross the fire. The floor is a lava lake; the path is a winding basalt causeway; the performers stand on obsidian islands 5 m off the path, read in procession as you pass.

  • Element beat: heat below and around you the whole way; ember rain.
  • Money shot: the defile's last corner, when the lake opens with three fire silhouettes doubled in the glow.
  • For: maximum drama per meter; straight reuse of LavaPool / LavaRivers / HeatDistortion; flat-floor tech, lowest risk.
  • Against: grammatically closest to Water (walkway between liquid, performers across the barrier). Same trick, hotter liquid.

B · The Kiln

You descend through the fire's breath. A tall round chamber, one spiral ledge dropping 6 m around a central lava well that flares on a slow rhythm. Performers on wall ledges at thirds, met face-on across the well.

  • Element beat: rhythm. The room breathes; between breaths it is nearly dark and the performance carries the light.
  • Money shot: the entry ledge, the whole kiln below, one full breath.
  • For: the cave's only vertical room; time as a medium; nothing in Water resembles it.
  • Against: hardest engineering (continuous helix elevation, not just stairs); framing performers across the well is fussy; biggest unknown at graybox.

C · The First Fire

You sit at the ancestral fire jam. The approach gets darker, not brighter. It opens into a stepped basalt amphitheater facing three performers across a lava fissure, each at a low fire pit, spinning burning props in the dark. The performance is the room's light source.

  • Element beat: fire as light. You feel its absence first; then the only light you get is the art.
  • Money shot: stepping from blackness into the row of benches as three sets of fire trails spiral against the dark.
  • For: this is what fire means to flow artists — the fire jam is the origin of the discipline. The pairs' opposite rotation senses are uniquely legible as trails: they draw two interleaved spirals. No other room can make the performance BE the lighting.
  • Against: darkness is risky at graybox (must obey "nothing 100% black"); prop-head fire + trails on museum performers is new work (effects exist, threading is new).

Recommendation — C's soul on A's approach

Build The First Fire, entered by a short lava crossing. Take Plan C and replace its plain dark crack with a compressed version of A's element beat: one tight basalt bridge over a lava stream, maybe 8 m, heat shimmer and ember rain, then the light behind you dies and you walk the last stretch toward a faint pulse of orange. The amphitheater reveal lands the way the surfacing stair did in Water: compression, then release.

Why C wins the room: Water's exhibit was performers behind the element. Copying that with lava (Plan A alone) would be a palette swap. C inverts the relationship: in the Fire room the element and the performance are the same thing. The automatons spin burning staves; the trails ARE the exhibit lighting; when a sequence rests between cycles the room dims with it. That is unique in the cave by construction, it is the most honest room in the museum (fire spinning in a circle of watchers is where this art form actually lives), and it makes the pedagogy visible: DJ's pro/pro opposite-spin draws mirrored spirals, EK's anti/anti reverses them, FL splits the difference one hand at a time. Each pair's trail goes out and comes home with the props locked 180° out of phase: one at the downbeat while the other crests, every step of the cycle.

The audience half: the stepped benches plus the cold ash circle with abandoned charred staves put the visitor inside the jam circle, not at a railing. The Order's fiction writes itself: the initiates surfaced from the water and dried themselves at this fire, and the first thing they did was spin.

Risk honesty: two new pieces of tech vs Water — (1) performance-synced lighting (room intensity follows the sequence player's beat clock), and (2) prop-head flames + trails on museum performer rigs. Both have existing ingredients (VolumetricFire, the trails effect family, the beat clock), neither is threaded into the museum yet. Graybox proves them with placeholder cones and emissive streaks before any art spend, same discipline as Water.
