Water · where we are

Three plans at one scale

Same metres per pixel in all three drawings, so the shapes are honestly comparable. Fire is the room that works. The traverse is what we built. The grotto is what I think Water should be.

Fire · Cinder Court

58 × 44 m room. Three courts, each entered through its own mouth. The performer is hidden until you cross the threshold.
Each performer owns a room. You arrive, you orbit, you leave. Nothing else is competing for your eye.

Traverse · what we built

56.5 m ribbon. Three daises sitting on the only path. A arrives 1.5 seconds in, and you can see it from the spawn point.
The dais is the only way to force a stop, so reverence reads as a roadblock. There is nowhere to stand and look that isn't the path itself.

Grotto · what I'd build

One room, one pool, three alcoves around it. Ice edge, drowned basin, steaming shallows. Reverence by framing across water, not by blocking the path.
The pool is the exhibit. Each performer is framed across still water and doubled by the reflection — and the apron is somewhere to stand that isn't in anyone's way.

The actual mistake

You asked for two things that were each right and that fight each other. "Make it the same length as Fire" — but Fire's 58 m is the width of a room, and I built a 58 m hallway. Then "each performer needs reverence, you can't walk past them" — and in a hallway the only way to stop someone is to stand in their way. So the dais became a roadblock. That's on me: I executed both instructions and never said they were pulling in opposite directions.

There's a deeper conflict underneath. The traverse's founding rule was open sightlines — "the end of the walk is visible from the beginning." Fire's founding rule is the opposite: performersHiddenUntilTheMouth is a checked, enforced property of its plan. You cannot bolt reverence onto a design whose whole thesis is that you can see everything at once and never need to stop.

So: are you over-correcting?

No. But I'd change one thing about the fix. Three separate chambers works and matches Fire, at the cost of building three rooms and losing the reflection pool everywhere except one of them. One grotto with one pool gets you the same reverence for a third of the build — and it keeps the single best idea the traverse ever had.

"A hot spring in a cold place is ice at the edges, liquid in the basin, steam at the interface." — that line is already in the traverse spec. It describes one pool, not three rooms.

One water surface, three relationships to it, is a real idea and it survives intact: A stands on the frozen edge, B is down in the drowned basin, C is in the steaming shallows. Same pool. You walk the apron around it, each performer is framed across still black water and doubled by their own reflection, and nobody is standing in your path. The sump entry stays — going under to get in is the one thing only this room can do to you, and it was already the approved reason the grotto lands when you surface into it.

What I'd keep from the last two weeks

The terrain module is throwaway — it encodes a corridor. What isn't throwaway: the measured-eye discipline (every clearance derived against a real 1.6 m eye), the on/under/in grammar, the waterline-crossing work, and the review bridge that lets a frame be captured without pointer lock. Those move to the grotto unchanged.
