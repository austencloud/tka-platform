Three stations, one grammar

The reverence rule made spatial: each performer stands on a platform the path is forced to acknowledge. The route (gold) swells around every dais and rejoins — you cannot pass A, B or C without walking around them. Same grammar three times, dressed by the element each time. Plan view, not to scale.

Station A · ice

The frozen pool

The river widens into a round frozen pool; A stands on a raised ice dais at its centre. The path circles either side and rejoins. First station in the museum — it sets the grammar: platform, walk around, watch.

Station B · sea

The trench dais

The channel splits around a rock dais rising from the trench floor, B on it under the god rays. Walking the ring means seeing the sequence from every side with the surface glittering overhead.

Station C · steam

The dais in the mirror

C stands on a vent platform ringed by the springs pool itself. The path wraps the pool rim, and the still water doubles the performer and their trails — reverence, the reflection, and dwell in a single move.

Why the same shape three times: repetition is what makes it a grammar instead of three set-pieces — by the third ring, walking around a platform means "a letter lives here." It also works in either room shape: three rings along the traverse, or rings for A and B on the approach with C's ring as the court's centre.
