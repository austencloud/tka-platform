Air — "The Chimney" floor-plan options

Bay ≈ 16 × 15 m, ~24 m authored height, daylight crack at +22. Enter north from Earth (−1.2), exit south to Sun (+8.4 → 0). Performers: DJDJ / EKEK / FLFL — Fire's pairs in tog timing, landing together. The question on this sheet: how much of the climb does the air itself do?

Plan A — Pure Switchback (concept doc as written)

Traversal: ~31 m of ramp, three landings, each ledge met at eye level. Wind = audio + rising motes only.

  • Pro: zero new tech; walker/clamp already proven on Fire's ramps.
  • Pro: serial eye-level meetings fully controlled.
  • Con: air never DOES anything — it stays scenery. Weakest element read in the wing.
Recommended

Plan B — The Last Lift (climb two, fly one)

Traversal: ramp to A and B as in Plan A; ramp 3 does not exist. The final +3.8 m is a visible updraft column — step in, the room lifts you past ledge C to the overlook. The lift is the reveal: during the ~4 s rise the three ledges stack into one vertical frame and land their shared beat as you crest.

  • Pro: air becomes the only element that MOVES the visitor — completes the wing grammar (under water, around fire, atop earth, carried by air).
  • Pro: one mechanic, one authored moment; no jumping skill required (walk in = lift). Failure surface tiny; fallback is restoring ramp 3.
  • Con: new walker tech: an updraft volume adding +y velocity with soft terminal speed + landing handoff. Must be prototyped first in graybox.

Plan C — The Updraft Shaft (full panel-hopping)

Traversal: a chain of updraft pads; each lift lands you on a floating step beside the next performer ledge. Air does 100% of the climbing.

  • Pro: the boldest element statement in the wing; pure "air carries you."
  • Con: whole room gates on lift/land feel; aim-while-floating is real platforming in a museum walker.
  • Con: accessibility — a fumbled hop strands the visitor at the floor; needs a rescue path anyway (which reinvents Plan B).

Recommendation

Plan B — The Last Lift. Keep the concept doc's serial eye-level climb for D-J and E-K (that pacing is the room's pedagogy: one mirror pair at a time), then delete ramp 3 and make the updraft the only way to finish. You get Plan C's magic exactly once, at the moment the room needs its reveal, on a mechanic simple enough to prototype in a day: a trigger volume with upward acceleration, soft terminal velocity, and a lip that catches you at the overlook.

Why the lift is the reveal: everywhere else in the wing the reveal is a threshold you walk through (Water's surfacing stair, Fire's crack, Earth's rim). Air's reveal should not be a place — it should be a state. For four seconds you are doing what the performers' props are doing: rising on a shared beat. You crest as all three pairs land together.

Graybox order: (1) prototype the updraft volume on a bare test shaft, gate on feel; (2) build the room. If the lift fails the feel gate, Plan A's ramp 3 is the drop-in fallback and nothing else moves.
