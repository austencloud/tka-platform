## 1. The ideal Fire → Earth → Air room, from first principles

I built this before judging the existing concept, from repository facts only.

### 1.1 What the cave actually is

`CAVE_MODE_ROOMS` in `vulcan-cave-floor-plan.ts` is not six themed caverns. It is a
six-station curriculum in timing and direction, and each room owns exactly one cell:

| Room | Category | Mode | Roster | Catalog family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water | SS | Split-time / same-direction | A, B, C | `tnd-split-same-*` |
| Fire | SO | Split-time / opposite-direction | DJ, EK, FL | `tnd-split-opp-*` |
| **Earth** | **TS** | **Together-time / same-direction** | **G, H, I** | `tnd-tog-same-*` |
| Air | TO | Together-time / opposite-direction | DJ, EK, FL | `tnd-tog-opp-*` |
| Sun | QS | Quarter-time / same-direction | S, T, U, V | `tnd-quarter-same-*` |
| Moon | QO | Quarter-time / opposite-direction | MP, NQ, OR | `tnd-quarter-opp-*` |

The 22-entry TnD catalog matches this table exactly. **Earth's subject is
Together-time / same-direction.** It is the first room in the cave where both hands move
at the same instant in the same direction — the most unified state the system ever
reaches. Everything before it is split timing; everything after it either opposes
direction or subdivides time.

The production contract never states this. Its Outcome sentence is *"G, H, and I share one
continuous hand route but produce different rotational structures."* That is the
*within-category* variation. The category itself — the thing this room exists to teach and
the only thing that distinguishes it from the other five — is absent from the contract, the
board, and the room.

### 1.2 What Fire hands over, and what Air takes

**Fire** (`2026-08-06-first-fire-torch-procession-design.md`, approved "I'm in, lock it",
tracker `jl8TveF5`) is a 60 × 30 m horizontal procession through a torch maze. Its design
rules are explicit: three shrines that **never share a performer sightline**, verified at
0.2 m visitor sampling. It ends with total extinction, one breath of near-silence, then
moss growing from a hidden gully, circling the last cold trench in green, and leading the
visitor around a bend.

That document also states what Earth owes it:

> "The existing Earth gully bend remains valuable because it hides the canyon until the
> visitor follows the green path around the corner."
> "…without spending Earth's canyon reveal early."

**Air** (`air-chimney-layout.ts`) is a 13 m vertical shaft (`AIR_CEILING_Y = 13.0`),
described in its own source as the narrowest plan and tallest space. Three performers sit
on ledges at three heights around the shaft; the visitor rides an updraft column past
them. The file's phrase for the intended frame is *"three heights, one beat."*

So the arc is: **Fire = isolated, one at a time, horizontal and hot. Air = all three at
once, vertical, in motion.**

This fixes Earth's brief by elimination. Earth must not repeat Fire's one-at-a-time
structure, and must not pre-spend Air's vertical-hero-with-three-performers-around-it
composition. Earth is the flat, wide, quiet exhale between a maze and a chimney — and it is
the room where "together" is the actual subject.

### 1.3 The free didactic nobody used

MCP and the catalog agree, and this is the most valuable thing I found:

- **G:** blue pro cw, red pro cw. Orientations `in → in` on all four steps.
- **H:** blue anti ccw, red anti ccw. Orientations alternate `in → out → in → out`.
- **I:** **red pro cw** (identical to both of G's hands) and **blue anti ccw**
  (identical to both of H's hands).
- All three: 4 steps, `tog` timing, `same` direction, start `beta5`,
  `handPathId: "s→w→n→e→s|s→w→n→e→s"`, `isCircular: true`, `loopType: "rotated"`,
  every step `turns: 0`, both hands together at beta throughout.

Two consequences the current design leaves on the table:

1. **I is not a metaphorical blend. I is literally G's red hand plus H's blue hand.** The
   board classifies "I combines both traces" as a *metaphor* (C-004 / manifest C-003). It
   is a **literal** per-hand identity, provable from the catalog. The strongest didactic in
   the room is currently filed as an authored interpretation.
2. **Because the three loops are step-for-step identical in hand path and timing, three
   performers on one shared clock are body-identical every frame.** Same arms, same
   positions, same beat. Only the props differ. A visitor who sees all three at once, in
   sync, learns the entire lesson in one glance with no caption, no metaphor, no invention
   and no trigger.

Pro at 0 turns is isolation, which sweeps a concentric ring; anti at 0 turns over a full
hand circle is a four-petal antispin flower. So the ring / petal / both vocabulary is
sound. It is just being delivered as floor decals on separated stages instead of as the
props in the visitor's eyeline.

### 1.4 The room this implies

**Working name: the Long Terrace.** This is an Opus proposal, not a decision.

One wide, shallow, brightly-lit shelf. The visitor arrives from Fire's darkness onto a
terrace that runs the room's full width. Below and in front, a single continuous
lower floor — not three habitats — holds G, H, and I standing **side by side on one
line, facing the terrace, running the same clock.** Beyond and below them the ground
falls away into the canyon that Fire's approved design already promises: shelves receding
into haze, no way down, weather and daylight from above.

Earth's hero is not an object. **Earth's hero is the row.** The image the visitor keeps is
three bodies moving as one body while three different shapes hang in the air in front of
them.

Why each choice:

- **Together, not isolated.** Fire owns isolation and proved it at 0.2 m sampling. Earth's
  category is Together-time. The room should be legible as a trio from the first step and
  spend its length on comparison, not on concealment. This is the single largest structural
  difference from the current concept and it is the one that makes the rest work.
- **Horizontal, not vertical.** Air owns the shaft. A 15 m hero under a 6.5 m ceiling is
  both a duplicate of Air and a geometry the room cannot render (§2.1). Earth's drama is
  breadth and distance — the canyon's haze — not height.
- **Bright, not dark.** Fire ends in blackout. Earth is the first daylight in the cave.
  The visitor should squint. Every current Earth frame is olive-on-olive at low contrast;
  the emotional reset from Fire is spent on nothing.
- **Performers are the brightest and largest thing in every frame.** First Fire achieves
  this in *primitives* — its shrine frames are hot coral against near-black with the
  performer at the centre of a lit ring. Earth's graybox inverts it: the floor decals and
  stage rims glow, and the performers are small dark silhouettes.
- **Close.** The comparison must be readable. A 1.7 m figure needs to be within ~8 m to
  subtend ~12° of frame height. The current climax puts them at 12.2–15.4 m
  (6.3°–7.9°, measured; §2.1).

Seven beats, in Gate 1 terms:

| # | Position and action | First focus | Response | Cue forward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emerge from Fire's green thread through a low throat | Daylight, not an object | Sound opens out; damp air | Brightness ahead |
| 2 | Step onto the terrace | The row of three, and the canyon behind them | Room reads whole in one frame | The near end of the terrace |
| 3 | Walk the terrace toward G | G's props draw concentric rings | The trio stays in frame; G is nearest | The row continues |
| 4 | Continue past H | H's props open four petals | Same bodies, different shape | The row continues |
| 5 | Continue past I | I's two props do both at once, in G's and H's own colours | The two shapes are recognised as borrowed | A near stand at the row's end |
| 6 | Reach the near stand, closest point on the line | All three at ~8 m, in sync, side by side | The three shapes hold together; the canyon opens behind | The Air fissure, ahead, not behind |
| 7 | Continue into the Air fissure | The rising slot | Ground drops away; updraft | Air |

Note beat 6→7: **the climax is on the way out, not down a spur.** The current route spends
28% of its length walking to a dead-end overlook and back (§2.1).

The retained-trace mechanic (C-005/C-006) survives as a **secondary** layer, applied to the
canyon face behind the row rather than to the floor: as the visitor walks, each performer's
shape etches into the far wall and stays. By beat 6 the three shapes hang together behind
the three bodies. It reinforces the comparison instead of substituting for it, and it
degrades safely — if a trigger is missed, the room still teaches, because the teaching is
the row.

### 1.5 On the canyon

`vulcan-cave-floor-plan.ts` describes the live room:

> "A grass gully opens onto the rim of a canyon. Three figures perform six metres below,
> on a floor there is no way down to; a fallen slab cantilevers over the drop and the
> shelves beyond recede into haze."

Three performers, below, unreachable, seen from a rim, with a cantilevered overlook, at a
6 m drop. That already solves the problems the Root Observatory keeps failing: the
performers sit far enough below that no route lip can eat them, the overlook is a real
destination, and the verticality is free.

The production contract's Authority ledger lists this under "Current conflict" as
*"Live description still names the Canyon Overlook"* and files the canyon as "reference
material." **It is not stale prose. It is a load-bearing dependency of an adjacent
approved design.** Fire's locked document names Earth's canyon reveal twice and shapes its
own ending around concealing it. Replacing a 6 m canyon with a 2.4 m basin silently
renegotiates an approved room's payoff, and no tracker item records that trade.

Tracker search is unambiguous: **zero items mention a canyon; the only item mentioning a
tree is `aN8CmfibQLjWC24E0lmJ`, the Gate 1 approval that the project itself records as
invalidated.** C-007 ("An ancient tree breaks through the roof and serves as the room's
visual hero") is the one load-bearing creative element with no tracker provenance, and its
claim-ledger status is `approved` on the strength of an approval the same document declares
void two sections later.

## 2. Root Observatory measured against that ideal

### 2.1 Measured findings

All numbers derived from `earth-root-observatory-plan.ts` in this session.

**(a) The room is not isolated — it is 59% ensemble, and it breaks at Stop 2.**

Sampling the walk path every 0.2 m (402 samples) and testing each performer against the
plan's own `isEarthRootObservatorySightlineBlocked`:

```text
visible-performer-count histogram [0,1,2,3]:  0 / 164 / 127 / 111
route seeing >= 2 performers: 59.2%
route seeing all 3:           27.6%
```

Per numbered stop:

```text
Stop 1 Fire threshold        sees: G
Stop 2 Tree reveal           sees: G, H, I      <-- all three, before any encounter
Stop 3 G habitat             sees: G
Stop 4 H habitat             sees: H
Stop 5 I habitat             sees: I
Stop 6 Recognition overlook  sees: G, H, I
Stop 7 Air exit              sees: G, I
```

The stated Outcome — "the visitor first encounters each performer alone" — is false from
the second stop onward, and the live Stop 2 capture confirms it: three performer bodies on
screen at the tree reveal. The Gate 1 `isolatedCloseReads` matrix passes because it samples
**only stops 3, 4 and 5**, the three most favourable points on the route. First Fire's
comparable standard is "at 0.2 m visitor sampling, no point on the route can see two
performer anchors." Earth fails Fire's own standard on 59% of its route.

I do not think Earth should meet Fire's standard — Together-time argues the opposite. But
the room currently claims isolation, is built around isolation, gates its climax on
isolation, and does not deliver it.

**(b) The Gate 1.1 amendment does not fix the reported defect.**

`earthRootObservatoryFloorSightlineMargin` evaluates the eye-to-feet ray at exactly
`routeWidth / 2`, at sample points on the route **centreline**. The reported margins
(G 0.24 m, H 0.42 m, I 0.31 m) are therefore centreline-only. The graybox terrain
(`createEarthRootObservatoryGrayboxTerrain`) allows the player anywhere within
`routeWidth / 2` of the polyline, so lateral offset up to 1.2 m is legal. Recomputing
across that legal band:

```text
lateral offset toward the wall |    G    |    H    |    I
                        0.0 m  |  +0.24  |  +0.36  |  +0.30
                        0.4 m  |  -0.03  |  +0.10  |  +0.03
                        0.8 m  |  -0.25  |  -0.12  |  -0.20
                        1.2 m  |  -0.43  |  -0.30  |  -0.40
```

(metres of foot-line clearance above the route surface; negative = feet hidden by the lip)

**G's feet are occluded 0.4 m off the centreline.** All three are occluded by 0.30–0.43 m
at the corridor edge. The rejection this amendment answers (`s278g83rbcQybhjZnIfO`) came
from **free walking**, which is precisely the condition the proof excludes. The amendment
proves a visitor who tracks the centreline within roughly ±0.35 m is fine. Nobody walks
like that.

Worse: `viewingWindow.startFraction` (0.25 / 0.35 / 0.35) is a tunable **inside the artifact
under test** that selects which part of the approach gets sampled. Raise it and any
approach passes. The pass criterion is parameterised by the thing that must pass.

**(c) The hero is decapitated at its own climax.**

From the final camera (walk path index 9, eye 2.20 m), with the plan's ceiling at 6.5 m and
`ceilingBreakRadius` 5.5 m:

```text
tree horizontal distance      12.17 m
angle to tree top (+15 m)     46.5 deg above horizon
highest visible tree point    10.05 m of 15 m
```

The ray to the canopy strikes the cave ceiling 8.1 m out from the trunk, well outside the
5.5 m aperture. **A third of the hero, including the canopy, is geometrically unreachable
from the stop where the room's stated payoff is "the canopy inclines toward the visitor."**
The live Stop 6 capture confirms: the trunk rises into a black void above a lit ring, and
the HUD caption promises a canopy the frame cannot show. The caption is doing the work the
geometry cannot.

The `final-view` check cannot catch this because
`isInsideEarthRootObservatoryFinalFrame` compares **horizontal bearings only**. There is no
vertical test anywhere in the plan module, for a 15 m hero in a 6.5 m room.

**(d) The ensemble does not read at the ensemble view.**

```text
G: 14.0 m,  bearing -34.8 deg (half-FOV 37.5),  apparent height 6.9 deg
H: 15.4 m,  bearing -13.1 deg,                  apparent height 6.3 deg
I: 12.2 m,  bearing +35.0 deg,                  apparent height 7.9 deg
```

G and I sit at 93% of the half-frame with 2.5–2.7° of margin — and that margin is measured
against a **centre point**, while their 2.2 m habitat discs subtend roughly ±9° and fall
outside the frame. At 6.3–7.9° apparent height the performers are about 90–110 px tall on a
1080p screen. The distinction between a ring, a four-petal flower and both — the entire
subject of the room — is not perceivable at that size. The live capture shows exactly this:
three small figures with coloured sticks.

Separately, the runtime first-person camera is `fovFirstPerson: 75` (camera-3d `types.ts`),
which Three.js treats as **vertical**; at 16:9 that is roughly 107° horizontal. So the plan
composed for a 75° horizontal frame and the runtime renders ~107°. The composition math and
the rendered frame are not the same frame.

**(e) The climax is at the end of a cul-de-sac.**

```text
overlook leg: 11.9 m out, 10.0 m back, then 4.7 m to the door
= 21.9 m of 79.4 m (28%) spent on an out-and-back
```

After the room's emotional peak the visitor turns around and retraces 10 m with the climax
behind them, then leaves. The Air threshold is not something you walk toward; it is
something you double back to.

**(f) The reveal is inverted.** A tree that has broken through to the surface should be the
only daylight in the cave. In both the Blender render and the live route the aperture is
the **darkest** region of the frame — a black void ringed with a thin green line. The
concept's central image argues against itself.

**(g) Composition at the climax.** From the live capture: the trunk is a featureless
cylinder bisecting the frame and physically separating H from I; a tan root fin cuts H's
stage; the parapet takes the bottom ~25% and empty dark ground another ~20%. Of the three
motif stages, only I's blue rosette reads as its motif — G's "ring" renders as a plain
green oval and H's petals are brown-on-brown against the roots. **The room's visual argument
is legible for one of three performers.**

### 2.2 What is genuinely good and must survive

- **The domain work is correct.** Every literal claim I re-checked through MCP and the
  catalog holds. C-001 through C-003 are accurate. The fingerprints in the manifest match
  the catalog file. This is the cleanest part of the package.
- **The claim ledger's literal/metaphor/invention discipline** is the single best idea in
  the whole workflow and should be exported to every museum room.
- **`verify-earth-root-observatory-glb.mjs`** is excellent: it parses the GLB header, rejects
  leaked `QA_`/`LOC_`/`REF_` nodes, rejects baked avatar nodes (mechanically enforcing
  `zf0pfUAmW4ODnjFJnep5`), and checks required node names, route segments, byte budget and
  extensions. This is what a real gate check looks like.
- **The shared-avatar seam was extended, not forked.** `showPlatform` and
  `standingSurfaceHeight` were added to `MuseumPerformerStation3D` so the room can supply
  its own stage without duplicating the performer system. Correct per `never-hand-roll.md`.
- **Plan-contract-as-code with a derived Blender contract** is the right architecture. The
  coordinate formulas are stated once and the drawing consumes the contract.
- **Preserving rejected evidence with its feedback** is the right instinct.
- **The Gate 1.1 board is a good drawing** — the rejected positions marked in red with X's,
  the numbered route strip, the literal/metaphor/invention legend. The problem is not
  craft.
- **Zero console errors** on every live capture.

### 2.3 The six assumptions that must change

Not object positions. Assumptions.

1. **"The 34 × 24 m shell is a given."** It was never derived. First Fire derived
   60 × 30 m from its experience and wrote a test to make the mismatch with the compiled
   46.5 × 20.5 m room explicit. Earth accepted 816 m² for a program comparable to Fire's
   1800 m² and then spent two gates fighting the consequences. Derive the shell, or state
   in writing that the shell is fixed and the program must shrink.
2. **"Isolation first, ensemble last."** That is Fire's structure, in the room whose
   category is Together-time, and the geometry does not deliver it anyway.
3. **"The room needs a vertical hero object."** It duplicates Air's composition one room
   early and cannot fit under a 6.5 m ceiling.
4. **"The environment carries the lesson."** The performers carry the lesson. Make them
   the largest and brightest thing in every frame; the environment reinforces.
5. **"The three loops are three separate exhibits."** They are step-for-step identical in
   hand path and timing. Sync them on one clock and the comparison becomes literal and
   free.
6. **"The canyon is stale reference material."** It is a named dependency of Fire's locked,
   approved design, and it already solves the sightline and verticality problems.

**Why restructure and not replace.** The domain spine, the claim discipline, the plan
contract pattern, the Blender bridge, the GLB verifier, the terrain program, the avatar
seam and the review route all survive a re-plan. Calling that "replace" would discard
working infrastructure. **Why not keep.** Gate 1.1 fails its own purpose and three of the
room's four headline promises — isolated reads, canopy recognition, ensemble legibility —
are contradicted by its own geometry.

## 3. Implementation fitness

**Sound.** Pure-function plan contract with no absolute world positions; a Blender contract
that derives rather than duplicates; the GLB verifier; the composition of
`MuseumPhysicsProvider` and `UnifiedCameraController` rather than forks; the shared-avatar
extension; scaling helpers that keep the plan room-relative.

**Gaps, in severity order.**

1. **The occluder that caused the failure is not in the plan.** The route lip exists
   nowhere as geometry — not in the plan contract, not in the terrain program (whose
   `elevationAt` returns the route elevation everywhere, so the physics floor is flat while
   the GLB shows a −2.4 m basin). It exists only as the scalar `routeWidth / 2` inside one
   sightline formula. The workflow's core promise — one coordinate contract for plan,
   Blender, GLB and runtime — is broken at exactly the point where it mattered.
2. **No state module.** Fire has `first-fire-procession-state.ts` with monotonic
   transitions, idempotent overlapping triggers, session persistence and reset, plus its
   own test file. Earth's C-005/C-006 describe reveal, persistence, convergence and a
   three-prerequisite gate — with no owner, no tests and no place in the plan. Fire's
   document warns directly against what will happen next: "Do not scatter progress
   booleans across geometry, scene, and performer components."
3. **No interaction robustness.** Fire specifies four 75° activation zones advancing every
   55°, giving 20° of overlap, explicitly so one missed boundary cannot strand the visitor.
   Earth has a single `interactionRadius: 3.2` and a recognition zone requiring all three
   prerequisites. A missed trace silently disables the climax with no recovery path.
4. **Two habitat types, one occlusion test.** `isEarthRootObservatorySightlineBlocked`
   consults `occluders` and the tree trunk. The five `habitatMasses` — up to 1.15 m of
   ferns — are never tested. Today they happen to sit clear of the sightline lanes. Nothing
   enforces that; a sixth field in the lane fails no check.
5. **Two divergent claim ledgers.** The contract carries C-001…C-009; the manifest carries
   C-001…C-005 for overlapping statements under different IDs and, for the ring/petal
   claim, a different class. The skill's own authority table demands one owner per truth.
   Nothing cross-references them.
6. **A 2D bearing test for a 3D composition.** No vertical framing check exists (§2.1c),
   and the plan's 75° horizontal assumption does not match the runtime's 75° vertical.
7. **Coordinate-space fragility.** `scalePoint` scales X and Z independently against a
   hardcoded nominal 34 × 24 while radii use `min(xScale, zScale)`. Correct only while the
   compiled room is exactly 34 × 24. The test asserts that, so a legitimate room resize
   fails as a test error rather than surfacing as a design decision.

**Tests.** The 12 passing tests are well written and genuinely constrain the roster,
route containment, grade and habitat area. They are also the clearest illustration of the
process's central weakness: they assert the properties the plan chose to expose, at the
sample points the plan chose to expose them. `expect(h.centre).toEqual({x: 15, z: 6.5})`
pins a coordinate; it does not test that the coordinate is good.

## 4. Process audit

### 4.1 Critical failures and slop paths

**S1 — Any check can be marked `passed` with a sentence.** The validator's only rule is
`isNonEmptyString(item.evidence)`. `{"name":"collision","status":"passed","evidence":"looks
fine"}` validates. This applies to every check in all seven gates, including `collision`,
`performance`, `runtime-console`, `focused-tests` and `typecheck`. This is the single
largest hole in the system.

**S2 — Any evidence requirement can be satisfied with a URL.** Path checking is skipped for
anything matching `^https?://`. `{"kind":"graybox-glb","path":"https://example.invalid/x"}`
passes. The validator's own self-test fixture uses `https://example.invalid/...` for every
evidence kind, which normalises the pattern.

**S3 — One file can satisfy four evidence kinds.** Kinds must be unique within a gate;
paths need not be. Earth's Gate 1 lists the same SVG as `floor-plan`, `vertical-section`
and `route-storyboard`. That is defensible for a genuine multi-panel board, and Earth's is
one. The validator cannot tell the difference between a multi-panel board and one file
listed three times.

**S4 — Regression evacuates the validated record.** `index > currentGate` forbids evidence
on later gates. When Gate 2 was rejected and `currentGate` moved back to 1, its entire
evidence package had to move into `revisionHistory` — a field the validator never reads. No
schema, no path checks, no digest verification, and it holds an `approval` object with no
`visualComprehensionConfirmed`. **The failure record is the least validated part of the
manifest.**

**S5 — The sightline study is its own proof.** Gate 1's `sightline-study` evidence is the
amendment report, and the `sightlines` check's evidence is a prose restatement of that same
report's numbers. The artifact asserting the check passed is the evidence that it passed.

**S6 — Self-selected sample points.** §2.1a and §2.1b. Isolation is proven at three chosen
stops; moving sightlines are proven along a window whose start fraction is a field in the
artifact under test. This is the mechanism by which Gate 1 passed twice on a room whose
first-person frames were wrong both times.

**S7 — Gate 0 `motion-proof` is a still image.** `visual-bridge.md` says "Capture continuous
motion as a loop. A still frame cannot prove timing," and Gate 0 requires capturing "each
live loop with a visible path diagnostic." Earth's `motion-proof` is the Gate 1 board SVG.
The validator cannot distinguish a loop capture from a drawing.

**S8 — The reviewer instructions break the repository.** The ledger directs reviews and
ledger edits into the Codex mirror tree, which `scripts/sync-codex-skills.mjs` generates
from the Claude skill source tree ("do not edit directly"). The ledger file *is* a managed
output, so a row written only in the mirror is overwritten by the next `pnpm skills:sync`.
This is not
hypothetical: `pnpm skills:check` **fails on `main` right now**, naming the ledger, the
Fable review and the sync manifest. And the ledger's own ratification criteria require
`pnpm skills:check` to pass. Following the instructions breaks the exit condition.

### 4.2 Gates: missing, redundant, too expensive

**Missing.**

- **A brief gate before Gate 1.** Gate 0 proves *facts*; Gate 1 presents a *solution*. There
  is no step in between where the room's job, its relationship to its neighbours, and its
  shell budget are agreed. Earth's whole failure is upstream of Gate 1: nobody ever wrote
  down that Earth teaches Together-time, that Fire owns isolation, that Air owns the shaft,
  or that 34 × 24 was a choice. **This is the highest-value change to the process.**
- **First-person frames at Gate 1.** Gate 1 approves a plan on top-down drawings, and the
  first eye-level frame arrives at Gate 2 after Blender. Every Earth fault was visible in
  the first eye-level frame. A crude WebGL fly-through from the plan contract — no Blender —
  would have caught all of it days earlier, for hours instead of days.
- **A vertical framing check.** Required wherever the hero exceeds ceiling height.
- **A sampled-route check, not a sampled-stop check.** Fire already does this at 0.2 m;
  promote it to a gate contract requirement.
- **A legibility check.** Minimum apparent size of the primary subject at every named
  camera. One line of trigonometry catches §2.1d.

**Redundant / too expensive.**

- **Gate 3 (registered visual target) as a separate human gate.** For a graybox-quality
  room whose materials are a small palette, the material/lighting brief can ride on Gate 2's
  approved cameras. Keep the camera registration rule; drop the separate approval.
- **Five distinct evidence kinds at Gate 2** encourages the one-file-many-kinds pattern
  (S3). Two would do: a playable build and a registered contact sheet.
- **Digest bookkeeping.** Hashes are recorded in the manifest, in the contract prose, in
  the reports and in the handoff. Four copies of the same fingerprint is three too many.
- **The handoff/contract/manifest/report/board quintet.** For Earth these overlap heavily
  and have already drifted (two claim ledgers, §3.5).

### 4.3 Visual bridge and comprehension check

The visual bridge is the strongest reference in the package, and Earth followed most of it.
Three real weaknesses:

1. **The diagnostic table has no row for "is the hero framable?"** It routes "What is the
   hero?" to a hierarchy diagram plus first and final hero views — but a hero view is a
   *drawing*, and Earth's tree passes as a drawing and fails as a frame. Add: *"Can the
   camera actually contain it? — Show the vertical section through the final camera, with
   the ceiling, the aperture and the ray to the hero's top."*
2. **The vertical-section requirement is not specified tightly enough.** Earth's "vertical
   proof" panel has cutaways for H and I, none for G (which has the worst margin), and no
   section through the final camera. It satisfies the requirement and misses the geometry
   that mattered. Require the section to pass through the final camera and every named
   reveal.
3. **The comprehension check tests recall, not prediction.** Its seven questions ask the
   user to describe a route they were just shown. Austen answered them correctly at Gate 1
   and the room was still wrong, because the questions never ask *"how big will each
   performer look?"*, *"what will you see above the hero?"*, *"where will you be standing
   when you leave?"* Add scale, framing and exit-direction questions. And a mismatch is
   correctly treated as an artifact failure — but a *match* is being treated as proof the
   design is right, which it is not.

### 4.4 Blender, runtime, domain, ownership

- **Blender.** Coordinate conversion is derived, collections are named, QA objects are
  excluded and mechanically verified. Good. Gap: the GLB is verified for *node presence*,
  never for *agreement with the plan* — no check confirms the GLB's stage centres match the
  contract's coordinates, so plan and geometry can silently diverge.
- **Runtime.** Composition over forking, zero console errors. Gap: the plan's camera model
  (75° horizontal) and the runtime's (75° vertical) are different cameras, and nothing
  reconciles them.
- **Domain.** The strongest area. Fingerprints, digests, MCP-in-turn discipline, and the
  refusal to substitute generated variants for museum sequences all held up under
  independent recheck. One correction: **the I = G-red + H-blue identity is literal, not a
  metaphor** — reclassify it and build on it.
- **Ownership.** Mostly correct and explicitly recorded. The two divergent claim ledgers
  (§3.5) violate the skill's own one-owner rule inside the skill's own scene.

### 4.5 Validator: bypasses and false rejections

Bypasses: S1, S2, S3, S4, S7 above, plus — `sequenceFingerprints[].fingerprint` is any
non-empty string and is never checked against the source file it claims to fingerprint;
only `sourceSha256` is verified, and all three Earth performers share one source digest, so
the per-sequence fingerprints are unverified strings.

False rejections and unhelpful strictness:

- **Regression destroys the record (S4).** A rejected later gate cannot keep its evidence in
  the validated structure. Add a `rejected` state that retains evidence and is exempt from
  the later-gates-must-be-empty rule.
- **`currentGate` is computed from completion only**, so a rejected gate and a
  never-started gate are indistinguishable.
- **No sub-gate model.** "Gate 1.1" exists in the prose, the filenames, the tracker and the
  board — and nowhere in the schema. The manifest can only say `measured-plan:
  ready-for-review` for the second time, with no record that this is a revision.
- **`--self-test` proves the validator's own fixtures**, six of which it authored. It does
  not test S1, S2 or S3, which are the failure modes that matter.

### 4.6 What must not change

The seven-gate ladder and its ordering. Human approval on 1–6 with a recorded quote and
tracker item. The refusal to infer approval from enthusiasm. The literal/metaphor/invention
classification. MCP-in-the-current-turn for every domain fact. Exact sequence sources over
generated variants. The visual bridge's diagnostic table. Preserving rejected evidence.
Plan-contract-as-code with a derived Blender contract. The GLB verifier.

### 4.7 The strongest counterargument to gated production

**Gates optimise for what is cheap to prove, and the things that decide whether a room is
good are expensive to prove.**

Earth is the demonstration. Twelve passing tests. A passing manifest. Six passing
self-tests. Zero console errors. Verified fingerprints and digests at every layer. And a
climax where the hero is decapitated by its own ceiling, the performers are ninety pixels
tall, and a caption promises a canopy the geometry cannot render. Every one of those faults
was visible in the first eye-level frame of the room. None of them was catchable by any
check in the contract, because they are questions of composition, scale, light and pacing —
and the gate ladder has no vocabulary for those.

Worse, the ladder actively displaced the effort. Two gates and roughly a dozen artifacts
went into proving ray clearances at chosen sample points, while nobody asked whether
34 × 24 m was the right room, whether Earth was repeating Fire's structure, or whether the
tree would fit in frame. A leaner loop — read the neighbouring room designs, build a crude
first-person blockout in an afternoon, walk it, iterate — would have surfaced all of it
sooner and cheaper. The process produced *confidence* faster than it produced *quality*,
and confidence without quality is the more dangerous of the two, because a passing manifest
reads as permission.

**The rebuttal, which I also believe.** The gates caught this at graybox, before a single
hour of lighting, material or art was spent, and they preserved a legible record of exactly
why. That is the system working. Ad-hoc iteration on this same room is what produced a
canyon nobody could account for and a tree with no tracker entry. The fix is not fewer
gates — it is moving the first eye-level frame earlier, and adding checks for the
properties that actually decide quality: apparent size, vertical framing, densely-sampled
route visibility, and whether the room's job is different from its neighbours'.

## 5. Who should do what

Assigned by demonstrated strength in this repository, not by branding.

| Work | Owner | Why, from evidence here |
|---|---|---|
| Room brief: what this room teaches, how it differs from its neighbours, what shell it needs | **Opus** (main loop) | The failure was cross-document reasoning: nobody joined `CAVE_MODE_ROOMS`, Fire's locked design, Air's shaft and the catalog. That is holding four documents at once, not producing one. |
| Adversarial geometry review of a plan before Gate 1 | **Opus** | Every finding in §2.1 came from re-deriving the plan's own numbers instead of reading its reports. This is the highest-leverage hour in the whole process. |
| Domain proof (MCP, catalog, fingerprints) | **Deterministic script + Opus check** | Already the strongest area, and it is mechanical. A script should emit the fingerprints; a model should only read them. |
| Plan contract, Blender contract, terrain, verifier, tests, exporters | **Codex or Sonnet from a written plan** | This code is good and it is deterministic transcription. It does not need the top tier. |
| Blender graybox construction and export | **Blender + scripts, driven by Codex** | The pipeline is scripted and verified; the GLB verifier makes the output checkable without judgment. |
| Prose: contract, board copy, handoffs | **Fable** | Long-form structured writing across many constraints. Note the current cost: five overlapping documents that have already drifted into two claim ledgers. Fewer documents, not better ones. |
| Looking at the room and deciding whether it is good | **Opus in a browser, then Austen** | Non-negotiable. Every fault in §2.1 was in the first frame. `visual-verification-mandatory.md` and `fable-routing.md` both already say a fan-out cannot see the page: *"the workflow sucked. do it yourSELF."* Earth is the second instance of that lesson. |
| Approval at every human gate | **Austen only** | Unchanged. |

**Design fan-outs and judge panels are the wrong tool for a room.** Multi-agent concept
tournaments produce documents; rooms are judged in frames. The evidence is in this
repository: the Earth package contains more written specification than First Fire and a
weaker room.

**What would justify changing ownership.** Move geometry review down a tier when a
Sonnet-tier pass independently reproduces §2.1(a)–(e) from the plan contract alone. Move
the room brief down a tier when two consecutive rooms are briefed without an
adjacent-room conflict like the canyon. Move visual judgment to a subagent only when a
subagent can render and read the frame itself — which is not today.

## 6. Recommendations, in priority order

**Process**

1. Make every `passed` check carry a verifiable artifact: a resolvable path plus a digest,
   or a command string with captured output. Kill the free-text pass. (S1)
2. Drop the `https?://` existence bypass, or restrict it to a whitelist of evidence kinds
   that genuinely live off-disk. Change the self-test fixtures off `example.invalid`. (S2)
3. Add **Gate 0.5, the room brief**: the room's teaching subject, its differences from both
   neighbours, a derived shell budget with the First Fire mismatch test, and a written
   statement of every dependency an adjacent approved design places on this room. One page.
   Human approval. This is the change that would have prevented Earth.
4. Move the first eye-level frame into Gate 1: a crude first-person fly-through generated
   from the plan contract, before Blender.
5. Add three checks to the contract: **vertical-framing** (hero and all subjects inside the
   real runtime FOV from every named camera), **subject-legibility** (minimum apparent
   angular height), and **route-sampled sightlines** at 0.2 m, promoted from Fire's
   precedent.
6. Forbid self-selected sample windows. Sampling parameters live in the check, never in the
   artifact under test. (S6)
7. Add a `rejected` gate state that retains its evidence inside the validated structure, and
   a first-class revision field, so `revisionHistory` stops being an unvalidated shadow
   schema. (S4)
8. Fix the ledger's own instructions: reviews and ledger edits go to the Claude skill
   source tree, then `pnpm skills:sync` propagates them. Get `pnpm skills:check` green
   before ratification. Also note that the mirror's platform-term transform rewrites the
   two tree names inside prose, so any text discussing this distinction must name the trees
   in words rather than as paths. (S8)
9. Require Gate 0 `motion-proof` to be a loop capture, and say so in a way the validator can
   check (file extension or a `mediaType` field). (S7)
10. Add scale, framing and exit-direction questions to the comprehension check, and stop
    treating a correct route recall as evidence the design is right. (§4.3)
11. Verify the GLB against the plan's coordinates, not only against node names.
12. One claim ledger per scene. The manifest owns it; the contract renders it.
