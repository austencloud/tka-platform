# Museum Spatial Design Reference

Real-world museum proportions, flow patterns, and layout principles. Derived from floor plan analysis of 9 major museums plus academic design research.

---

## The One Rule: Compression and Release

The single most important spatial design principle. Narrow corridors opening into grand halls amplifies the narrative. Every museum that feels memorable uses this.

- **Compression:** narrow corridors (3-4 tiles wide), low implied ceilings, dim lighting, small rooms
- **Release:** grand halls (40+ tiles wide), high implied ceilings, bright light, open space
- The transition between them creates "aha" moments
- Our current layout has rooms all roughly the same size — that's why it feels flat

## Six Layout Types (Naredi-Rainer)

1. **Open Plan** — large open spaces, free wandering
2. **Linear Chain** — rooms in sequence, one after another
3. **Round Tour (Loop)** — circular path returning to start
4. **Core and Satellite** — central hub with rooms branching off
5. **Labyrinth** — maze-like, intentionally disorienting
6. **Complex/Hybrid** — most real museums combine types

**For The Kinetic Archive:** Hybrid of core-and-satellite (macro) with labyrinthine (micro). MJT-inspired intimate chaos inside a classical museum shell.

## Museum of Jurassic Technology (Tone Reference)

- ~1,100 sq m total (tiny — about 44 × 25 tiles)
- ~20 rooms across 2 floors
- Narrow corridors you can barely pass someone in (1-2 tiles)
- Near-total darkness as atmosphere
- Hidden doorways behind curtains
- Sound leaking between rooms as wayfinding
- Each room is its own complete world
- No published map (disorientation is the design)
- Impossible interior courtyard (shouldn't fit but does)

## Key Chicago Museums

### Art Institute of Chicago
- 1 million sq ft across interconnected buildings (100+ year accretion)
- Modern Wing: 264,000 sq ft (Renzo Piano)
- Mix of intimate enfilade galleries and sweeping modern spaces
- Straddles open-air railroad tracks

### Field Museum
- 700 ft long × 350 ft deep, cruciform plan
- Stanley Field Hall: 300 × 70 ft, 80 ft high (the "release" space)
- Hub-and-spoke from central hall into symmetric wings

### Museum of Science and Industry
- 14 acres exhibit space, cross-shaped with central dome
- 1893 World's Columbian Exposition building
- Contains a full submarine underground

## Design Rules for Our Museum

1. **Vary room sizes dramatically.** Small → large → small again. No two adjacent rooms the same size.
2. **Corridors should be tight.** 3-4 tiles wide maximum. The tightness makes rooms feel spacious by contrast.
3. **At least one grand space.** A central hall or rotunda that serves as the orientation landmark.
4. **Hidden rooms.** At least one room behind a non-obvious passage (MJT principle).
5. **Aspect ratios matter.** Long narrow galleries (3:1) feel like journeys. Square rooms feel like destinations.
6. **Transitional pauses.** Brief widening at room boundaries where visitors reorient.
7. **Sound and darkness as wayfinding.** Not just visual — atmosphere guides the player.
